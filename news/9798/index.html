Ex-condo chairman loses defamation suit over undisclosed links

2026/10/31

A former condominium chairman has lost a defamation case connected to links that were not disclosed. The details of the dispute belong to that case. The plain lesson is about openness.

People who help run a condo are expected to say so when they have a personal or business link that could affect a decision, such as who the estate hires. Hiding that link can turn into a legal fight, not just an argument in the chat group.

For someone buying a condo, how the estate is run can matter as much as the renovation inside the unit. Disputes can show up later as fees, delayed repairs, or a tense atmosphere.

3 Takeaways for Buyers & Sellers

  • Before you buy a condo, ask about monthly fees, upcoming works, and any dispute you should know about. A nice unit in a poorly run estate can become expensive.
  • If you sit on your estate’s council, say so when you have a link to a contractor or a decision. Keeping it quiet can become a much bigger problem.
  • Sellers should be ready for questions about unpaid fees and big repair bills. A clear answer is better than a surprise after the option is signed.
