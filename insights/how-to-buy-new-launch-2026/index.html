How to Buy a New Launch Condo in Singapore: A Step-by-Step 2026 Guide

To buy a new launch condo in Singapore, you'll: (1) get your finances and loan approval ready, (2) register interest and ballot for the project, (3) pick your unit on launch day and pay a 5% booking fee, (4) exercise the Option to Purchase (OTP) within the deadline, (5) pay stamp duties, and (6) follow the Progressive Payment Scheme until you collect keys at TOP. The whole journey runs from a few weeks to 3–4 years, because you're buying a property that is still being built.

In this guide, you'll learn each step in order, the exact costs and deadlines to plan for, the documents you'll need, and the common mistakes that cost first-time buyers their booking fee or their dream unit.

Quick Answer: The New Launch Buying Process at a Glance

  1. Sort your finances — In-Principle Approval (IPA) and cash for the booking fee
  2. Engage an agent and register interest in the project
  3. Attend the launch / balloting and select your unit
  4. Pay the 5% booking fee and receive the Option to Purchase (OTP)
  5. Exercise the OTP (typically within 3 weeks) and pay the next portion
  6. Pay Buyer's Stamp Duty (and ABSD if applicable) within 14 days of exercising
  7. Sign the Sale & Purchase Agreement (S&P) within the statutory window
  8. Follow the Progressive Payment Scheme through construction
  9. Collect keys at TOP, with final payment at CSC

Now let's walk through each stage in detail.

Step 1: Get Your Finances Ready Before You Step Into a Showflat

The biggest mistake buyers make is falling for a unit before they know what they can afford. Sort this first.

Check Your Loan Eligibility (TDSR and LTV)

  • TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio): Your total monthly debt obligations generally can't exceed 55% of your gross monthly income.
  • LTV (Loan-to-Value): A first bank-financed housing loan is typically capped at 75% of the price or valuation, with the first 5% of the down payment payable in cash (verify current LTV split with MAS / your bank before relying on it).

Get an In-Principle Approval (IPA)

An IPA is a bank's non-binding indication of how much it will lend you. Always secure your IPA before launch day — on a hot launch, you won't have time to sort financing after you've chosen a unit, and an unexercised booking fee can be forfeited.

Calculate Your Cash on Hand

You'll need ready cash for:

  • 5% booking fee (cash, on the day)
  • Balance of the down payment (cash + CPF, due shortly after)
  • Buyer's Stamp Duty (and ABSD if applicable)
  • Legal fees, typically $2,500–$3,500

Step 2: Engage an Agent and Register Your Interest

For new launches, the developer appoints marketing agencies, and the buyer typically doesn't pay the agent's commission — it's borne by the developer. This means you can engage a salesperson to represent your interest at no direct cost.

A good agent will:

  • Send you the e-brochure, floor plans, and price list ahead of launch
  • Advise on stack, facing, and unit selection strategy
  • Register you for VIP preview / priority balloting
  • Walk you through the paperwork and deadlines

Register your interest early. Priority previews and balloting positions often favour those who registered ahead of the public launch.

Step 3: Attend the Launch and Select Your Unit

On launch day (or the VIP preview), units are released and selected in order — often by ballot or queue position.

How Unit Selection Works

  • The developer releases units according to a balloting or queue system.
  • When your number is called, you have a limited window to confirm your unit choice.
  • Popular stacks go fast, so have a first, second, and third choice ready by stack and floor.

What to Prioritise When Choosing a Unit

  • Facing and orientation — afternoon sun, noise, and views materially affect resale value.
  • Stack — corner units, sea/greenery-facing, or low-traffic stacks tend to hold value better.
  • Layout efficiency — a well-planned smaller unit can live larger than a bigger inefficient one.
  • Quantum — the total price you can comfortably service, not just the PSF.

Step 4: Pay the Booking Fee and Receive the Option to Purchase (OTP)

Once you confirm your unit, you pay the booking fee — typically 5% of the purchase price — and the developer issues you the Option to Purchase (OTP).

  • The booking fee is usually paid by cheque or cashier's order (confirm accepted methods with the developer).
  • The OTP gives you the exclusive right to buy that specific unit for a fixed period.
  • The developer then sends the Sale & Purchase Agreement (S&P), usually within a couple of weeks.

At this stage you've reserved your unit — but you haven't committed to completing yet.

Step 5: Exercise the OTP (or Walk Away)

You now have a decision window — typically around 3 weeks from receiving the S&P — to exercise the option.

If You Exercise

You sign the S&P Agreement and pay the next portion of the down payment (so your total typically reaches the standard down-payment level). You're now contractually committed to the purchase.

If You Don't Exercise

You forfeit a portion of the booking fee (commonly 25% of the 5% booking fee, i.e. 1.25% of the price — verify with the specific developer's terms). The unit is released back to the developer.

This is why your financing must be locked before you book. Booking on hope and failing to exercise is an expensive lesson.

Step 6: Pay Your Stamp Duties

After exercising the OTP, stamp duties fall due — generally within 14 days of exercising the option (verify current deadline with IRAS).

Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)

BSD applies to every residential purchase on a progressive scale based on price or value, whichever is higher. Higher-value tiers attract higher rates (verify the current bands and rates with IRAS before publishing).

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD), If Applicable

ABSD depends on your profile and how many residential properties you already own:

  • Singapore Citizens: 0% on the first property; higher rates on the second and subsequent (as of 2026 — verify with IRAS).
  • PRs and foreigners: higher tiers apply.
  • ABSD remission may be available for married couples buying a replacement matrimonial home, subject to strict timing rules on selling the first property.

Step 7: Follow the Progressive Payment Scheme (PPS)

Because a new launch is a Building Under Construction (BUC), you pay in stages tied to construction milestones — not in a single lump sum.

The key benefit: your mortgage instalments start small and grow as the bank disburses the loan in stages, easing cash flow during the 3–4 year build.

Step 8: Collect Your Keys at TOP

When the project receives its Temporary Occupation Permit (TOP), you can collect keys, inspect your unit, and move in or rent it out.

What to Do at Key Collection

  • Inspect thoroughly and document every defect (a defects checklist is essential).
  • Submit your defects list within the developer's defects liability period (commonly 12 months from TOP).
  • The developer is obliged to rectify legitimate defects within that period.

Final legal completion follows at CSC (Certificate of Statutory Completion), when the last payment is made and the title is fully transferred in due course.

Common Mistakes When Buying a New Launch

  1. No IPA before launch day. You'll lose your unit — or your booking fee — if financing isn't ready.
  2. Booking on emotion. Stack, facing, and layout matter more than showflat styling.
  3. Underestimating ABSD timing. Upgraders buying before selling can face heavy ABSD unless remission timing is hit precisely.
  4. Ignoring the defects window. Document everything at key collection — the liability period is finite.
  5. Forgetting the fit-out budget. "New" doesn't mean move-in-ready; you'll still spend on curtains, lighting, and built-ins.
  6. Choosing on PSF alone. A low PSF on a poor layout gives you less usable, sellable space.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much deposit do I need to buy a new launch condo? +

You'll need a 5% booking fee in cash on the day you select your unit, then top up to the standard down payment (commonly around 20%, payable via cash and CPF) when you exercise the OTP. Stamp duties and legal fees are on top of this.

Do I pay agent commission when buying a new launch? +

Generally no. For new launches, the developer pays the marketing agency's commission, so you can engage a salesperson to represent you at no direct cost.

How long does it take to buy a new launch condo? +

The booking-to-commitment stage takes a few weeks (booking fee, then exercising the OTP within ~3 weeks). But because it's a Building Under Construction, you typically wait 3–4 years to collect keys at TOP.

What happens if I don't exercise the Option to Purchase? +

You forfeit a portion of your booking fee — commonly 25% of the 5% (i.e. about 1.25% of the price) — and the unit returns to the developer. Confirm the exact terms in your OTP.

Can I use CPF to buy a new launch condo? +

Yes, subject to CPF housing rules and limits. CPF can fund part of the down payment and monthly instalments, though the first 5% booking fee is paid in cash. Check your CPF Ordinary Account balance and usage limits before you commit.

When do I start paying my mortgage for a new launch? +

Under the Progressive Payment Scheme, the bank disburses your loan in stages as construction progresses, so instalments start small and increase over the build period — easing cash flow compared with a resale lump-sum purchase.

What is the difference between TOP and CSC? +

TOP (Temporary Occupation Permit) means the building is safe to occupy — you collect keys and can move in. CSC (Certificate of Statutory Completion) is the final legal sign-off, when the last payment is made and statutory requirements are fully met.

Is buying a new launch riskier than resale? +

There are different risks: new launches carry off-plan risk (TOP delays, market shifts during the build), while resale carries hidden-defect and ageing-facility risk. Neither is universally riskier — it depends on your timeline and risk appetite.

Should HDB upgraders choose new launch condos or resale in 2026? +

Most HDB upgraders with equity and 5+ year horizon benefit from new launch condos because the progressive payment scheme aligns cash flow with HDB sale, and you capture new precinct growth. Choose resale if you must move in fast or need rental income immediately. See our dedicated new launch vs resale guides.

Final Word: Preparation Beats Speed

Buying a new launch isn't about being fastest on launch day — it's about being fully prepared before you walk in. Lock your financing, know your budget down to the stamp duty, have your stack choices ranked, and understand every deadline. Buyers who do this walk away with the unit they wanted at a price they planned for. Buyers who improvise lose deposits.
