Walking Distance to Sixth Avenue MRT from Dunearn House

Depending on which block you're in, you're looking at roughly 4–10 minutes on foot to Sixth Avenue MRT. Realistically, for the average resident, ~7 minutes is a fair benchmark.

That's not "MRT-integrated" close, but it's well within the comfort zone that most Singapore families look for — close enough that kids can walk it independently, far enough that you're not sitting on top of the station.

What Sixth Avenue MRT Gets You

Sixth Avenue is on the Downtown Line (DTL) — one of Singapore's most useful lines for families:

  • Botanic Gardens — 1 stop (weekend family outings)
  • Newton — 3 stops (Newton interchange to NSL)
  • Bugis — 7 stops (without transfer)
  • Downtown / CBD — ~20 minutes door-to-door
  • Bayfront — direct, no transfers (for that Marina Bay Sands weekend)

For school-going kids, that's also direct access to MGS, Singapore Chinese Girls' School, and Catholic High clusters depending on which stop they alight at.

And in the Near Future: Dual-Line Access

The Turf City MRT station on the Cross Island Line (CRL Phase 2) is expected around 2032. When it opens, Dunearn House becomes a dual-line address — Downtown Line at Sixth Avenue, plus Cross Island Line at Turf City.

That's a big deal for families. Dual-line access typically means shorter commutes, more route options, and a much easier daily life when school runs and work commutes don't align.

For Drivers

If you're a two-car family (common in this area), you're equally well-served:

  • PIE entry: ~5 minutes
  • BKE entry: ~5 minutes
  • Orchard Road: ~10–15 minutes
  • CBD via Dunearn → Bukit Timah → River Valley: ~15–20 minutes off-peak

Is Dunearn House Good for Families with Children?

Yes — and the unit mix tells you the developer designed it that way.

The Unit Types Are Built for Growing Families

Unlike many CCR launches that load up on shoebox units for investors, Dunearn House skews heavily toward:

  • 2-Bedroom — for young couples planning kids or empty-nesters right-sizing
  • 3-Bedroom — the family workhorse (kids' rooms + master)
  • 4-Bedroom and 4-Bedroom + Study — for bigger families, multi-gen households, or families who want a dedicated study/WFH room

There are reportedly no 1-bedroom units. That's a deliberate choice — it means you won't be living next to a rotating cast of short-term tenants, which is a real quality-of-life win.

Low-Density = More Breathing Room

~380 units across ~145,000 sqft of land = roughly 380 sqft of land per unit at the site level. By Singapore standards, that's spacious. Translation:

  • Less queue for the pool
  • More room between blocks
  • Better cross-ventilation and natural light
  • Quieter common areas
  • More mature landscaping over time

Designed for Daily Family Routines

A low-rise, garden-style condo with 35%+ greenery cover means kids can actually play outdoors, residents can stroll the grounds, and you don't need to "go somewhere" to get fresh air. For young children, this is huge.

Common Concerns Families Bring Up (And Honest Answers)

"Won't construction noise from Turf City be terrible?"

It will be present, especially in the first few years post-TOP. Selecting stacks facing inward or away from active construction zones helps a lot. By 2032–2035, much of the noise will have moved on as the broader masterplan completes.

"What about traffic on Dunearn Road?"

Dunearn Road is a major arterial. Higher floors and certain orientations face the traffic side; quieter stacks face away. Stack selection at booking really matters — don't rush this.

"Is the school 1km eligibility guaranteed?"

No. OneMap gives you a preliminary view, but final eligibility depends on MOE's measurement during your child's registration year. Always verify closer to actual registration.

"How does it compare to staying in a freehold landed home in Bukit Timah?"

A landed home gives you space and tenure. A condo gives you facilities, security, lower maintenance, and easier resale. Many families upgrade from landed to condo as kids grow up because the day-to-day is simpler.

"Will the masterplan actually deliver?"

URA-backed masterplans in Singapore have historically delivered, though sometimes with timeline slippage. Punggol, Bidadari, and Bishan all materialized. Turf City is a 20-year story — you should expect progressive delivery, not overnight transformation.

Conclusion: Is Dunearn House the Right Family Home?

If you've been searching for a family condo that delivers on schools, MRT, greenery, and long-term liveability all at once — Dunearn House is one of the most genuinely family-suitable new launches of 2026.

It's not perfect. It's a 99-year leasehold in a freehold-heavy area, you'll wait until 2030–2031 to move in, and there'll be construction around you for several years. Those are real trade-offs.

But the upside is rare: a low-density CCR family condo, in the heart of Singapore's top education belt, with an MRT station 7 minutes away (and a second one coming), wrapped in nature, with a strong developer team.

For families thinking 10–15 years ahead — kids growing through PSLE, O-Levels, JC, and beyond — this kind of location is what you're really paying for.
