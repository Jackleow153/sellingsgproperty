Are New Launch Condos Cheaper Than Resale? A 2026 Singapore Buyer's Guide

Short answer: no — new launch condos are usually more expensive than resale on a per-square-foot (PSF) basis, often by 15%–35% in the same area. But that headline hides the part that actually matters to your wallet: new launches typically demand far less cash upfront thanks to the progressive payment scheme, and their total cost over time can work out differently once you factor in renovation, holding costs, and capital growth.

In this guide, you'll learn why new launches command a price premium, when that premium is worth paying, the situations where resale wins outright, and how to compare the two on total cost — not just sticker PSF.

Quick Answer: New Launch vs Resale Pricing in 2026

  • On PSF: Resale is almost always cheaper. New launches in the same precinct typically sell at a 15%–35% premium (verify current gap with URA Realis / EdgeProp before publishing).
  • On upfront cash: New launches usually win, because the Progressive Payment Scheme (PPS) spreads payment over the 3–4 year construction period.
  • On total cost: It depends. Add renovation (often $50k–$120k for resale), holding costs, and projected capital growth before you decide.
  • On move-in timing: Resale wins — you can move in within ~3 months versus waiting 3–4 years for TOP.

So "cheaper" is the wrong question. The right question is: cheaper on what — PSF, upfront cash, or total cost over my holding period?

Why New Launch Condos Cost More Per Square Foot

There are concrete reasons new launches carry a premium. Understanding them helps you judge whether the premium is justified.

1. Higher Land and Construction Costs

Developers buy land at today's prices and build at today's construction costs, which reset sharply higher after 2022 and have largely stayed elevated. That break-even cost gets baked into the launch PSF. A resale unit completed in, say, 2016 was built on a cheaper cost base.

2. Brand-New Condition (Zero Renovation)

You are paying for fresh finishes, new appliances, and a fresh 99-year (or freehold) clock. A resale unit usually needs $50k–$120k+ of renovation to feel new, which narrows the gap once you account for it.

3. The "New" Premium and Developer Marketing

New launches are marketed heavily, sold in a controlled environment (showflats, staged pricing, launch-day momentum), and priced for the developer's margin. Buyers also pay for the optionality of being early in a precinct's transformation.

4. Latest Layouts and Facilities

Newer projects often have more efficient layouts, smarter unit mixes, and updated facilities (co-working lounges, larger gyms, smart-home features) that older developments lack.

Why Resale Condos Are Usually Cheaper on PSF

The flip side explains the discount:

  • Older cost base. Built and sold at earlier price levels.
  • Visible wear. Ageing finishes, older facilities, and dated layouts.
  • Renovation needed. The lower price often reflects work the buyer must fund.
  • Lease decay. For 99-year leasehold, a shorter remaining lease pulls the price down — and matters a lot once the balance dips toward 60 years.
  • Larger, less efficient floor plates. Older units are often physically bigger, which can mean a lower PSF but a higher total quantum.

The Catch: Upfront Cash Tells a Different Story

This is where many buyers get the picture wrong. Even though a new launch has a higher PSF, it usually needs less cash today, because of the Progressive Payment Scheme.

How the Progressive Payment Scheme Works (Simplified)

With a new launch bought under construction (BUC), you pay in stages tied to building milestones:

  1. Booking fee (typically 5%, in cash) on the option to purchase
  2. Down payment to top up to the standard amount, due within weeks (subject to LTV rules)
  3. Progressive disbursements as the project hits each construction stage (foundation, walls, roofing, etc.)
  4. Final payments at TOP and CSC

Because your loan is disbursed in stages, your monthly mortgage starts small and grows as construction progresses — easing cash flow during the build.

Resale: Lump-Sum Reality

With resale, you typically complete within ~12 weeks, which means:

  • Full down payment due upfront
  • Full mortgage instalments begin almost immediately
  • Renovation cash needed before you can move in

So a resale unit with a lower PSF can still demand more cash in your first 12–18 months than a new launch with a higher PSF.

When a New Launch Is Worth the Premium

Pay the premium when:

  • You can wait 3–4 years for keys and don't need to move in now.
  • Your cash flow is tight today but improving — PPS spreads the strain.
  • You believe in the precinct's transformation (new MRT lines, masterplan upgrades, en-bloc-driven renewal in areas like Bayshore, Lentor, or Marina South — verify launch status before publishing).
  • You want zero renovation hassle and a fresh lease.
  • You're an investor betting on capital appreciation from launch price to TOP and beyond.

When Resale Wins Outright

Choose resale when:

  • You need to move in within 6 months.
  • You want immediate rental income rather than a 3–4 year wait.
  • You value space and proven layouts — older units are often larger and more efficient.
  • You want a known product with no off-plan, TOP-delay, or market-shift-during-build risk.
  • You're buying in a mature estate where comparable transacted data makes pricing transparent and negotiable.

Consider two hypothetical 3-bedroom units in the same OCR precinct (illustrative figures — replace with current transacted data before publishing):

  • Resale unit: $1.7m at a lower PSF, but needs ~$80k renovation, full mortgage from month one, and lump-sum completion.
  • New launch unit: $1.95m at a higher PSF, but under PPS you pay the booking fee and staged down payment, with mortgage instalments starting small and ramping up over 3–4 years.

On PSF, the resale is clearly cheaper. But in the first 18 months, the new launch buyer may part with significantly less cash, because they're not funding renovation, not servicing a full mortgage, and not completing in a lump sum. Whether the resale's lower price ultimately wins depends on your holding period and the precinct's capital growth.

This is exactly why "are new launch condos cheaper than resale?" can't be answered with a yes or no until you define your timeframe.

How to Compare the Two Properly (5 Steps)

  1. Pull transacted PSF for both new launch and resale in the same precinct over the last 6–12 months (URA Realis / EdgeProp).
  2. Calculate the real premium — not the brochure price, the actual gap versus comparable resale.
  3. Add renovation to the resale's effective cost.
  4. Map your cash flow month by month for the first 24 months under each option.
  5. Project your exit — estimate capital growth and net proceeds for your expected holding period, after SSD if applicable.

If you do all five, the "cheaper" option usually becomes obvious for your situation — which is the only situation that matters.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Are new launch condos always more expensive than resale? +

On a PSF basis, almost always yes — typically a 15%–35% premium in the same area (verify the current gap with URA data). But on upfront cash required in the first year, new launches are often cheaper because of the Progressive Payment Scheme.

Why are new launch condos so expensive in 2026? +

Three main reasons: higher land prices paid by developers, elevated construction costs that reset upward after 2022, and the brand-new "premium" of fresh finishes plus a full lease term. Marketing and developer margins also factor in.

Is it cheaper to buy a resale condo and renovate? +

Sometimes, but not always. A resale unit's lower price is often partly offset by $50k–$120k+ of renovation plus immediate full mortgage payments. Once you add these, the gap to a new launch narrows considerably.

Do new launch and resale condos have the same stamp duty? +

Yes. Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) and, where applicable, Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) follow the same rules regardless of whether the unit is new or resale (verify current rates and bands with IRAS).

Which appreciates faster, new launch or resale? +

New launches in transforming precincts often show stronger capital appreciation from launch to TOP, but they also carry more uncertainty. Resale in mature estates tends to appreciate more steadily and predictably. Neither is universally better.

Can I negotiate the price of a new launch condo? +

Generally there's limited room — developers control pricing and release units in stages. Resale, by contrast, is usually negotiable since you're dealing with an individual seller and transparent comparable data.

New launch condos or resale condos — which has better resale value later? +

New launches in masterplan areas (e.g. Turf City, Jurong Lake District) often see the biggest uplift from launch price to TOP + early resale because they capture the infrastructure premium first. Mature resale estates have steadier but smaller step-ups. Liquidity is usually better for established resale addresses.

Final Word: "Cheaper" Depends on Your Timeline and Cash Flow

If you're comparing only the price tag, resale is usually cheaper per square foot. But once you account for upfront cash, renovation, holding period, and capital growth, the answer genuinely flips depending on your circumstances.

The smart move isn't to ask which is cheaper in general — it's to model both options against your budget, timeline, and goals.
