Quick Answer

No, not reliably. 8 Singapore mall condos grew an average 2.6% a year from launch to 2026, while nearby condos of similar size grew 3.2% a year. I compared each of the 8 completed mall condos with 2 or 3 neighbours (20 in all). The 3.2% uses the 17 neighbours whose launch price is known; prices run to November 2026. The mall condo beat all its neighbours in only 1 of 8 areas. What decided the winner was not the mall but how much more the mall condo cost at launch, and when in the market cycle it launched.

"Integrated" condos, built on top of a mall and usually next to an MRT station, are sold as the safest new launch you can buy. Convenience is real, and so is the price premium. The question for a buyer is simple: does the premium come back to you when you sell?

This article answers that with sale records, not opinions. For each mall condo I picked 2 to 3 condos nearby of similar size, then compared launch price, MRT distance, developer, completion year and yearly price growth. For every condo, I also explain why it did better or worse.

Data as of November 2026. Prices are yearly averages from URA caveats via the Stacked Homes condo directory; launch prices are from launch reports. Past performance does not predict future prices.

Key takeaway: Where the mall condo launched 15% or more above its neighbours, the neighbours grew faster in 5 of 7 areas. The one clear win, The Poiz Residences, was the only mall condo that launched cheaper than the condos around it.

Five reasons that explain most of the results

  1. The launch premium. A mall condo that starts 20% to 50% dearer needs its neighbours to stand still to keep its lead. They rarely did. Bedok Residences launched 35% to 47% above Waterfront Isle and Archipelago, and was only 2% higher five years later.
  2. Market timing. Condos launched in 2011 to 2013 (Bedok Residences, Urban Vista, The Venue Residences, Sennett Residence) were bought near a peak, then hit the 2013 loan limits; most were flat for five years. 2015 launches (The Poiz Residences, Symphony Suites) were bought near a low. 2018 to 2019 launches rode the 2020 to 2023 rise.
  3. How close the neighbours are to the same MRT. At Woodleigh, Punggol and Potong Pasir the neighbours sit within 500 m of the same station, so the mall condo's transport edge was small. At Yishun the neighbours are 1.4 km and more from the MRT, and North Park Residences held level.
  4. Too much new supply at once. North Park Residences (920 homes) and Watertown (992) stayed flat until a few years after completion, when hundreds of owners could sell at the same time.
  5. Unit design and mix. Urban Vista had small 3-bedders, loft units and road and train noise. It did worst of all 28 condos in this study.

Where the mall condo did hold up

  • It still sells for more per square foot than its neighbours in 6 of 8 areas. The exceptions: Parc Esta now sells above Park Place Residences, and Parc Centros has edged past Watertown.
  • On a 1,000 sq ft home bought at launch, North Park Residences (about $489k) and The Poiz Residences (about $672k) gained more in dollars to November 2026 than any of their neighbours.
  • Over the first five years, Hillion Residences beat Eco Sanctuary (2.7% against 1.4% a year), helped by the Bukit Panjang Downtown Line station that opened next door in December 2015.

What other studies found

  • Stacked Homes (May 2025): across 17 mall condos, the average total gain was 29.9% over 2,300 deals, against 32.4% for other condos over 157,181 deals. For buyers who bought new and sold at resale: 30.2% against 29.5%.
  • 99.co (2021): no clear, guaranteed advantage in price growth or rental return; scarcity was the only clear edge.
  • EdgeProp (May 2025): concluded mall condos tend to give sellers higher profits, citing the largest single deals. Watertown had 377 profitable resales and 33 at a loss; Compass Heights 515 and 89.
  • A PropNex agent's guide (Oct 2026): the extra price tends to hold when the gap to the nearest condo is about 7% to 10%, and resale gets harder at 20% to 30%. The figures in this study agree.

What this means if you are looking at a mall condo now

  1. Compare the launch price with the nearest condos first. Under about 10% above them, mall condos have held value well. At 20% or more, expect the neighbours to catch up.
  2. Check where the neighbours are. If they are also within 500 m of the same MRT, you are paying mainly for the mall.
  3. Check the market timing. A launch priced at a market high starts with less room to grow, mall or not.
  4. Count the supply. A 900-plus-unit project completing at the same time as its neighbours can stay flat for years after completion.
  5. Plan to hold, not to sell in a collective sale. Shop owners in the same development can block a collective sale, and running costs are higher with two maintenance funds.

Mall condos on this site that this applies to now include Lucerne Grand, Pinery Residences, Parktown Residence, Chencharu Central Residences and Hougang Central. Each project page lists the nearby condos to compare against.

Frequently asked questions

Do integrated condos with a mall appreciate faster in Singapore?

Not reliably. Across 8 completed mall condos and 20 nearby condos of similar size, the mall condos grew about 2.6% a year from launch to November 2026, against about 3.2% for the neighbours with a known launch price. The mall condo beat all its neighbours in only 1 of 8 areas.

Why did cheaper nearby condos often grow faster than the mall condo?

Mostly because they started from a lower price. Mall condos in this study launched up to 35% above their neighbours. Market timing, how close the neighbours were to the same MRT station, and large amounts of supply completing at once also mattered.

Is a mall condo still worth buying?

It can be, for own stay, when the launch premium over the nearest comparable condos is small (about 10% or less) and the neighbours are not close to the same MRT station. The Poiz Residences, which launched below its neighbours' prices, was the best performer in its area.

Do mall condos earn higher rent?

Not clearly. In this study the mall condo's gross rental yield was higher in 6 of 8 pairs, usually by only 0.1 to 0.4 percentage points, and lower at North Park Residences and Sengkang Grand Residences.

How was this study measured?

Yearly growth from the reported launch price to the November 2026 average price per square foot in URA caveat records. It is a price comparison, not each owner's actual profit, and leaves out stamp duty, loan interest, fees and rent.
