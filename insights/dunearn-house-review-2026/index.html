If you're reading this, chances are you've already seen the buzz around Dunearn House — the first private condo to launch within the new Bukit Timah Turf City masterplan. And you probably have one question: is it actually worth the money?

Short answer: Dunearn House looks like a strong long-term buy for families and CCR upgraders who can stomach a 99-year leasehold at around $2.8k–$3.1k psf. It sits in a low-supply prime pocket, walking distance to Sixth Avenue MRT, with first-mover positioning in a 20–30 year transformation story. But it's not for everyone — especially investors hunting for quick rental yield or buyers who need a freehold.

Below, we'll break down everything you need to know — pricing, location, schools, developer track record, and the honest pros and cons — so you can decide if it's the right fit for you.

Who's Behind Dunearn House? The Developer Story

This is one part of the project I think buyers underrate.

Dunearn House is being built by a joint venture of three serious players:

  • Frasers Property — Singapore-listed, decades of track record (think Riviere, Sky Eden, One Bernam).
  • Sekisui House — Japan's largest homebuilder, over 2.6 million homes built globally. Known for quality detailing.
  • CSC Land Group — part of China State Construction, one of the world's largest builders.

That mix matters. Sekisui House in particular is known for tight construction tolerances and finishing quality — which historically command a small premium on resale in the Singapore market.

If you've been burned by a smaller developer before (cracks, defects, delayed handover), this consortium is about as safe as you can realistically get.

Dunearn House Location Review: What's Really There?

Let's be honest — "Bukit Timah" is one of those addresses that sounds prime, but the reality depends a lot on which exact pocket you're in. Here's what you actually get:

Connectivity (The Strong Point)

  • Sixth Avenue MRT (Downtown Line) — roughly a 4–10 minute walk, depending on which block.
  • Direct trains to Newton, Bugis, Downtown, Bayfront — no transfers needed for the CBD.
  • Future Turf City MRT (Cross Island Line Phase 2) — expected around 2032, will add a second line right at your doorstep.
  • PIE and BKE within ~5 minutes by car.

For a CCR address, this is genuinely well-connected. Many older Bukit Timah condos sit awkwardly between MRT stations — Dunearn House does not.

Schools (The Family Magnet)

The Bukit Timah school belt is the single biggest reason families pay a premium here. Schools commonly cited within 1–2km include:

Primary:

  • Methodist Girls' Primary (within 1km, per OneMap — but verify closer to launch)
  • Nanyang Primary
  • Pei Hwa Presbyterian Primary
  • Raffles Girls' Primary

Secondary / JC:

  • Nanyang Girls' High
  • Hwa Chong Institution
  • National Junior College
  • Methodist Girls' Secondary

Always verify final P1 distance eligibility with MOE OneMap closer to registration — boundaries can be tight at stack level.

Lifestyle & Nature

You're sandwiched between Bukit Timah Nature Reserve, Singapore Botanic Gardens, and the upcoming Bukit Timah–Rochor Green Corridor (a linear park along the old railway canal). For people who actually use parks — joggers, cyclists, families with kids — this is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade.

For food and groceries, Greenwood Avenue, Coronation Plaza, Bukit Timah Plaza, and Cold Storage Sixth Avenue are all close by. It's not as buzzy as Holland Village, but it's quieter — which is exactly why people pay to live here.

Dunearn House Pros and Cons (My Honest Take)

This is where I'll try to be straight with you, not salesy.

✅ The Pros

1. First-Mover in Turf City Transformation

You're buying at the very start of a 20–30 year masterplan. Selected heritage Grandstand buildings will be conserved as lifestyle clusters. As infrastructure rolls out (MRT, parks, amenities), early entrants typically see the strongest capital appreciation.

2. Walking Distance to Sixth Avenue MRT

This is non-negotiable for most Singapore buyers today. A future second line (CRL) only strengthens this.

3. Top-Tier Schools Within Reach

Multiple primary schools within 1–2km. This is the kind of factor that holds value through every property downcycle.

4. Reputable Developer Consortium

Frasers + Sekisui + CSC is about as bankable as it gets for build quality.

5. Low-Density, Low-Rise Design

Only ~380 units on 145,000+ sqft of land. That's spacious by Singapore standards and means less competition for facilities (and on resale, less internal supply pressure).

6. CCR Pricing That Looks Reasonable (For Now)

$2,800–$3,100 psf is significantly below recent CCR comparables. If you've been priced out of D9/D10 prime, this is one of the few rational entry points left.

❌ The Cons

1. 99-Year Leasehold

Bukit Timah purists will tell you this area is "freehold country." A 99-year lease in a Good Class Bungalow neighbourhood feels slightly out of place, and lease decay will eventually matter.

2. Construction Dust for the Next Decade

Turf City is a masterplan, not a finished neighbourhood. Expect ongoing construction, road works, and noise around you until at least 2030–2035. First few years of living there may be less serene than the brochures suggest.

3. Family-Heavy Unit Mix = Lower Rental Yield

There are reportedly no 1-bedroom units, and 3BR/4BR dominate. Great for own-stay, but rental yields on bigger units in CCR typically run 2.5%–3% — not the 4%+ you'd see in OCR studios.

4. Quantum Is Still High

A 3BR at ~$2.8M is no joke. You need substantial CPF/cash, and ABSD will hit hard if this is a second property (20% for Singapore citizens on second property as of 2026).

5. The Adjacent Project Could Cannibalize Resale

The next Dunearn Road plot (Wing Tai/Metro) is right next door. When it launches at a higher psf, it could either lift your value or compete with your resale, depending on timing.

6. TOP Only in 2030–2031

That's 4–5 years of progressive payment, plus you can't move in or rent it out anytime soon. Capital is locked in.

💡 2026 Key Takeaway

First-mover GLS projects in masterplan precincts have historically outperformed later launches in the same area. Dunearn House is in that pattern — but only if you have the horizon and stomach for the leasehold and construction period.

Is Dunearn House a Good Investment?

Here's how I'd frame it:

  • For own-stay family buyers: Probably yes, if school proximity and the long-term Turf City story matter to you. The location is genuinely strong and the developer is solid.
  • For pure investors chasing yield: Probably no. CCR rental yields are mediocre, and the 99-year lease in a freehold belt limits upside slightly.
  • For long-term capital appreciation hunters: Cautiously yes. You're betting on (a) Turf City actually delivering its masterplan, (b) CRL Phase 2 opening, and (c) Bukit Timah continuing to hold its premium. All three are reasonable bets — but bets nonetheless.

Who Should Buy Dunearn House?

Based on the unit mix and pricing, this project is clearly designed for:

  • Young families wanting to settle near the Bukit Timah school belt
  • HDB upgraders with strong CPF/cash buffers (think $2.5M+ comfortable quantum)
  • Right-sizers moving from larger landed homes in the area
  • Long-term CCR believers who want first-mover entry into Turf City

It's probably not the right fit for:

  • First-time buyers looking for an entry-level property
  • Pure rental investors chasing yield
  • Anyone needing to move in within 1–2 years
  • Buyers who specifically want freehold

A Few Common Mistakes to Avoid

After speaking to many buyers in similar situations, here are the slip-ups I keep seeing:

  1. Stretching too hard on quantum. Just because the bank can lend doesn't mean you should max out TDSR. Always stress-test at 4%+ interest rates.
  2. Assuming P1 priority is guaranteed. Within-1km doesn't mean automatic admission. Verify with MOE closer to actual registration year.
  3. Ignoring ABSD on second properties. A 20% ABSD on a $3M unit is $600,000. That changes the math entirely.
  4. Forgetting progressive payment. From booking to TOP (2030/31), you'll be paying down progressively. Plan cashflow accordingly.
  5. Comparing only headline PSF. Layout efficiency, stack orientation, and floor level matter as much as PSF. A poorly-oriented 1,000 sqft unit at $2,800 psf is worse than a well-oriented one at $3,000 psf.

Conclusion: Should You Buy Dunearn House?

If you're a family planning to live here, with the financial buffer to handle a CCR 99-year leasehold and the patience to ride out 4–5 years until TOP plus another 5–10 years of Turf City construction — yes, Dunearn House is one of the more strategically interesting launches of 2026.

If you're chasing yield, freehold, or quick flips — look elsewhere.

The honest truth is that there's no "perfect" property in Singapore right now. But Dunearn House offers a rare combination: a strong location, reputable developers, a masterplan tailwind, and CCR pricing that — for once — looks more reasonable than the alternatives.

My next step suggestion: Get the floor plans the moment they're released, walk the actual site, and book a showflat slot during VVIP preview if possible. The first wave usually gets the best stacks and earliest-bird discounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When is the Dunearn House launch date? +

The VVIP preview is expected around July 2026 (2H 2026), with public launch shortly after. Dates may shift — always confirm directly with the developer or an accredited agent.

What is the expected Dunearn House price? +

Based on the land cost of $1,410 psf ppr and current market commentary, the launch price is estimated at ~$2,800–$3,100 psf. Indicative quantums range from ~$2M (2BR) to ~$4.5M (4BR+Study).

Who is the Dunearn House developer? +

Dunearn House is jointly developed by Frasers Property, Sekisui House, and CSC Land Group, under the entity Phoenix Dunearn Pte Ltd.

How far is Dunearn House from Sixth Avenue MRT? +

The development is approximately a 4–10 minute walk to Sixth Avenue MRT on the Downtown Line, depending on which block you're in.

Is Dunearn House freehold or leasehold? +

It is a 99-year leasehold development on a Government Land Sales (GLS) site.

Is Dunearn House suitable for families? +

Yes. The unit mix focuses on 2-Bedroom to 4-Bedroom + Study layouts with no 1-bedroom units, and it sits within reach of multiple top schools including Methodist Girls', Nanyang Primary, Hwa Chong, and Nanyang Girls' High.

Is Dunearn House a good investment? +

For long-term capital appreciation and own-stay, yes — first-mover positioning in the Turf City masterplan and limited CCR supply support upside. For pure rental yield, it's less ideal, as CCR family-sized units typically yield 2.5–3%.

What is the Bukit Timah Turf City transformation? +

It's a large-scale URA masterplan to redevelop the former Turf Club into a new residential precinct, with conserved heritage Grandstand buildings, parks, community amenities, and a future Cross Island Line MRT station (Turf City MRT). Dunearn House is the first private residential launch within this transformation zone.
