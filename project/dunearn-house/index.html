Why Dunearn House Is Generating Genuine Attention in 2026

1. It Is the First Private Launch in the Turf City Precinct

Bukit Timah Turf City is a multi-decade urban regeneration plan that transforms the former Singapore Turf Club racecourse grounds into a new residential, retail, and lifestyle precinct — government-backed, master-planned, and supported by a new Turf City MRT station already under construction. Dunearn House is the first residential development inside this precinct.

First-mover positions in transforming Singapore precincts have historically delivered outsized returns. Buyers who entered Punggol's first private launches in the early 2010s, or Lentor's first projects in 2022, captured the precinct repricing that followed. The structural ingredients are present: new MRT connectivity, government-backed masterplan, and constrained land supply within the surrounding GCB enclaves and protected forest reserves.

2. The Developer Consortium Is Globally Branded

Frasers Property (SGX-listed, S$40B+ asset base) — Singapore's third-largest property group, with deep experience in residential, commercial, and integrated developments. Sekisui House (TYO-listed, Japan's largest residential homebuilder) — global construction quality standards and expertise in sustainable design. CSC Land Group — Singapore arm of China State Construction Engineering, one of the world's largest contractors. For a project that does not TOP until late 2030, this consortium is about as low-risk on construction execution as Singapore offers.

3. The GLS Tender Validated the Precinct

When the URA released the Dunearn Road GLS site, nine developers tendered, with the top five bids tightly clustered (gap between top and second-highest bid was just 3.7%). In a 2026 GLS market where some sites have attracted only 2–4 bids, nine is a strong signal of broad developer conviction.

The Five Honest Concerns Every Smart Buyer Has

Concern #1

"Why pay S$3,000+ psf for a 99-year leasehold when freeholds are right there?"

Nearby Royalgreen (freehold, by Allgreen) currently transacts at around S$2,934 psf. Dunearn House is likely to launch at S$2,900–S$3,100 psf for a 99-year project — almost no gap to freehold. That is unusual, reflecting compressed freehold premiums in 2026 driven by 60% ABSD thinning the foreign buyer pool and resident end-users prioritising location over tenure structure.

For a buyer with a 7–15 year hold horizon, the practical lease decay impact is minimal. Fresh 99-year tenure retains 84 years by 2040. Where freehold genuinely matters: ultra-long-term hold (25+ years) or inter-generational planning. For most upgraders and investors, entry pricing and precinct trajectory are the variables that determine outcome — not tenure.

Concern #4

"The wait to TOP is too long."

Dunearn House TOPs in December 2030 — more than four years from purchase. Three factors mitigate the execution risk: a three-developer consortium spreading financial risk across three globally-listed entities; Singapore's new Land Sales Disqualification Framework (effective 22 May 2026) imposing up to 5-year bans on developers delivering defective projects; and the Progressive Payment Scheme structuring your cash outflows against construction milestones, not upfront. The four-year wait is also strategically useful — it locks in today's land cost basis while the surrounding Turf City precinct develops around you.

Concern #5

"What about competition from other D11 launches?"

D11 supply is structurally constrained — only 2 GLS plots in this cycle on Dunearn Road; the next supply requires fresh GLS designation (typically 3–5 years). Plot 2 will launch at higher PSF (projected S$3,150–S$3,300 by CBRE and SRI) due to its 15.2% higher land cost — positioning Dunearn House as the relatively more affordable option in the precinct. Existing comparables (Fourth Avenue, Royalgreen, Leedon Green) are different products for different buyer pools. Competition is real but bounded.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dunearn House overpriced at S$2,900–S$3,100 psf?
Not on the comparable evidence. The adjacent Plot 2 won at 15.2% higher land cost and is projected to launch at S$3,150–S$3,300 psf, positioning Dunearn House as the more affordable option in the precinct. Breakeven is ~S$2,400–S$2,550 psf — a 15–20% developer margin, thin by prime district standards. For long-horizon SC first-property buyers, the pricing is well-supported by location, schools, and the Turf City catalyst.
Is the 99-year leasehold a problem in a prime district?
Only marginally, and only over very long holds. Fresh 99-year tenure retains substantial value for the first 30 years before lease decay meaningfully impacts resale. A fresh 2025 lease still retains 84 years in 2040. For 7–15 year hold horizons, the lease decay impact is small. Freehold genuinely matters for 25+ year holds or inter-generational planning — for most upgraders and investors, entry pricing and precinct trajectory determine outcome more than tenure.
How does Dunearn House compare to Fourth Avenue Residences?
Fourth Avenue launched into a pre-COVID, cooling-measure-heavy 2018–2019 market with no precinct catalyst — current avg S$2,522 psf reflects that. Dunearn House launches into a stabilising 2026 market with the Turf City masterplan first-mover advantage, a stronger three-developer consortium, and a structurally lower land cost base. Fourth Avenue is the cautionary tale of paying prime-district price with no transformation story. Dunearn House has both the prime address and the fresh catalyst.
Why does the land cost gap to Plot 2 matter?
Because it establishes Dunearn House as the structurally cheaper entry in the precinct. Dunearn House won at S$1,410 psf ppr (July 2025); Plot 2 sold at S$1,625 psf ppr (May 2026) — 15.2% higher. The Frasers/Sekisui/CSC consortium tried to win Plot 2 and was outbid. Future launches on Plot 2 need higher PSFs to make their numbers work, giving Dunearn House a permanent relative-value advantage as the precinct's first mover.
What is the expected rental yield for Dunearn House?
Gross yield projection: 2.8–3.4% based on Fourth Avenue Residences (3.4%) and Dunearn 386 (3.2%) comparables (EdgeProp, May 2026). Net yield after maintenance, tax, and vacancy: 1.8–2.4%. The tenant pool — international school families, finance and legal professionals, embassy staff, NUS faculty — pays premium rents for the D11 lifestyle and school catchment. Yields are highest on 2-bedroom units.
