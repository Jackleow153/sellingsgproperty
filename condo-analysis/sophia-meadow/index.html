Sophia Meadow: analysis and verdict

132 Sophia Road (Mount Sophia) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,950 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,110 psf as a new launch). 2-bed is the most wanted size in this rental and investment area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed at or below $3,110 psf; a 3-bed of about 840 sq ft is about $2.61M.
  • Best size here: 2-bed: this is an rental and investment area, where smaller units suit investors and tenants.
  • Walk away: Above $3,270 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.75M). The current average $2,950 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Sophia Hills ($2,237 psf, 3-bed size, last 24 months (6)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,270 psf.
  • Watch: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Sophia Hills, The Collective at One Sophia, Cairnhill Nine) work out to about $3,110 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (29% to 34% here): launches selling now in the CCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out). A 3-bed here at about $2,945 psf is $708 psf (+32%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $2.35M.
  • Also note: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) site nearby (1,059 m, land $1,865 psf ppr) is likely to launch at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf: more than this project's price.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the current average $2,945 psf, the base case makes about +10% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (2.5% a year); bought at $3,110 psf it makes about +4%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.47M; about $2.90M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders yes; HDB 4-room upgraders yes; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: wide.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: moderate. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Moderate concern)

Besides this project's own 41 homes, about 3,075 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Duet@Emily, The Collective at One Sophia, Peck Hay Road (GLS), Dorset Gardens); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 1,244 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Expect some competition when units complete together, mainly on rents and resale speed, but no collapse: the local buyer pool is deep.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

0% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with many units left, late discounts or deals on slower stacks are possible as the deadline nears, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS), is likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
