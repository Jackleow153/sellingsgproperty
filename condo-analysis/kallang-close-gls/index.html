Kallang Close (GLS): analysis and verdict

Kallang Close (Kallang River) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Walk away above $2,940 psf. A 3-bed here at about $3,000 to $3,100 psf is 9% above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,800 psf as a new launch). Buy only at or below about $2,800 psf. 2-bed is the most wanted size in this rental and investment area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,800 psf; a 3-bed of about 950 sq ft is about $2.66M.
  • Best size here: 2-bed: this is an rental and investment area, where smaller units suit investors and tenants.
  • Walk away: Above $2,940 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.79M). The expected $3,000 to $3,100 psf is above that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Sturdee Residences ($2,238 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,940 psf.
  • Watch: The Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. A cheaper new launch may come nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 470 homes, about 1,136 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Aurea, Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS), Dorset Gardens); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 727 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (High concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Moderate concern)

The next launch nearby, Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS), is likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf, close to this project's 3-bed. A cheaper neighbour later could cap this project's resale price; compare both before committing.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year), widened to the nearest suitable condos: Kerrisdale (1.0 km): Eight Riversuites 4.6%, Kerrisdale 6.3%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.4% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): Kallang MRT is already open and no new Confirmed catalyst is due in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
