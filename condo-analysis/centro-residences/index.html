Centro Residences: analysis and verdict

Ang Mo Kio, D20 · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy only below $2,070 psf. A 3-bed here at about $1,980 psf is a little above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $1,970 psf as a 12-year-old resale like this one), so negotiate or choose a cheaper stack. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this own-stay family area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $1,970 psf; a 3-bed of about 905 sq ft is about $1.78M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an own-stay family area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,070 psf (a 3-bed above about $1.87M). The recent resale average $1,980 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Grandeur 8 ($1,392 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (4)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,070 psf.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 329 homes, no other new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it; the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 172 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Moderate concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

No other land site nearby is waiting to launch, so there is no newer, cheaper launch to wait for here.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

  • Rental yield at this price: 3.0% gross. Competitors' median rent about $5.01 psf a month (URA rental contracts, 26q1, 26q2, 26q3, 26q4); a 3-bed rents for about $5,300 a month.
  • New supply within 2 km: 0 projects. No other project launched since 2024 or coming within 2 km.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): Grandeur 8 4.0%, Clover By The Park 7.6%, The Panorama 5.5%, Sky Vue 6.4%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.9% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (5.7% a year): Base plus 1 point a year for the Cross Island Line (Phase 1) at Ang Mo Kio station, Confirmed for 2030.
