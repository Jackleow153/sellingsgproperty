Dairy Farm Walk (2026 GLS site): analysis and verdict

Dairy Farm Walk (Dairy Farm) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,150 to $2,300 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,260 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this own-stay family area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,260 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,000 sq ft is about $2.26M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an own-stay family area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,370 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.37M). The expected $2,150 to $2,300 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Dairy Farm Residences ($1,698 psf, 3-bed size estimated from its other sizes) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,370 psf.

Key points

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 480 homes, about 540 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Narra Residences); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 403 resales a year. Our test of 33 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the outer suburbs (11 km or more from the CBD): those with more than 1,817 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 4.0% a year over the past 4 years, against 4.3% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

This site's land ($962 psf ppr) cost −6% more than Narra Residences's ($1,020), which lifts the developer's break-even by about −4%. A 3-bed here at about $2,225 psf is +2% above Narra Residences's $2,180 launch average; private prices have risen about +1% since then (URA index), so in today's money you pay about 1% more than its buyers did, about as much as the developer's higher costs: the land bid is being passed on. Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

No other land site nearby is waiting to launch, so there is no newer, cheaper launch to wait for here.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): The Skywoods 4.5%, Foresque Residences 5.9%, Eco Sanctuary 6.0%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.5% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site: Hillview MRT on the Downtown Line has been open since 2015, so the bull case equals the base case.
