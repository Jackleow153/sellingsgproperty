Nava Grove: analysis and verdict

38–42 Pine Grove · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Sold out: buy only as a sub-sale or a resale, at or below about $2,710 psf. At launch: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,340 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,580 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,580 psf; a 3-bed of about 970 sq ft is about $2.50M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,710 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.63M). The launch average $2,340 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Clavon ($2,249 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (5)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,710 psf.
  • Watch: The Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site) launch nearby: likely at about $3,150 psf (our estimate). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 552 homes, about 492 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site), Amberwood at Holland); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 437 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Low concern)

99.8% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with most units sold, there is little pressure to cut prices.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site), is likely at about $3,150 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): The Trilinq 4.5%, The Clement Canopy 5.2%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 4.8% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
