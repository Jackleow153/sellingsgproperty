Arina East Residences: analysis and verdict

Tanjong Rhu Road · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,880 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,090 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $3,090 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,105 sq ft is about $3.41M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $3,240 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.58M). The launch average $2,880 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Fulcrum ($2,037 psf, 3-bed size, last 24 months (4)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,240 psf.
  • Watch: The Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Fulcrum, The Line @ Tanjong Rhu, One Meyer, 8m Residences, Meyer Mansion) work out to about $3,090 psf as a new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (8% to 29% here): launches selling now in the RCR charge about 3% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: Fulcrum (102 m away, 128 homes, completed 2016): its 3-bed-size resales (about 830 to 1,380 sq ft) averaged $2,037 psf over the last 24 months (4 resales), against a median $2,231 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,880 psf is $843 psf (+41%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $2.85M.
  • Also note: The Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS) site nearby (888 m, land $1,455 psf ppr) is likely to launch at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf: more than this project's price.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the launch average $2,880 psf, the base case makes about +17% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (4.0% a year); bought at $3,090 psf it makes about +9%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $3.18M; about $3.97M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders with some savings; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing yes; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: good.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 107 homes, about 1,592 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Meyer Blue, Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS), Emerald of Katong); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 641 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Low concern)

94% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with most units sold, there is little pressure to cut prices.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS), is likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
