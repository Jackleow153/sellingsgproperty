Meyer Blue: analysis and verdict

83 Meyer Road · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy only below $3,330 psf. A 3-bed here at about $3,210 psf is a little above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,170 psf as a new launch), so negotiate or choose a cheaper stack. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $3,170 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,140 sq ft is about $3.61M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $3,330 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.80M). The current average $3,210 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Meyer Mansion ($2,804 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (6)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,330 psf.
  • Watch: The Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. A cheaper new launch may come nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 226 homes, about 953 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Arina East Residences, Emerald of Katong); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 595 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Moderate concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Low concern)

81% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with most units sold, there is little pressure to cut prices.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Moderate concern)

The next launch nearby, Tanjong Rhu Road (GLS), is likely at about $2,900 to $3,000 psf, below this project's 3-bed. A cheaper neighbour later could cap this project's resale price; compare both before committing.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
