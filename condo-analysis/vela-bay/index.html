Vela Bay: analysis and verdict

Bayshore Road / Bayshore Walk (Bayshore precinct, Bedok) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Walk away above $2,740 psf. A 3-bed here at about $2,860 psf is 10% above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,610 psf as a new launch). Buy only at or below about $2,610 psf. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,610 psf; a 3-bed of about 960 sq ft is about $2.51M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,740 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.63M). The current average $2,860 psf is above that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Eco ($1,706 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (7)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,740 psf.
  • Watch: The New Upper Changi Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,230 psf (our estimate). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Eco, Grandeur Park Residences, Seaside Residences, Urban Vista, The Glades) work out to about $2,610 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (21% to 34% here): launches selling now in the OCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.5 points more for each year of its age (29 launches against 102 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: Eco (1,362 m away, 748 homes, completed 2017): its 3-bed-size resales (about 820 to 1,100 sq ft) averaged $1,706 psf over the last 6 months (7 resales), against a median $1,571 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,865 psf is $1,159 psf (+68%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $1.80M.
  • Land bids nearby: The newest land bid nearby, New Upper Changi Road (GLS) (1,885 m, $1,537 psf ppr), is 11% above this site's land cost. It breaks even at about $2,540 psf, and is likely to launch at about $3,230 psf (break-even plus the 27% that launches since 2023 priced above theirs).
  • Potential gain: Bought at the current average $2,865 psf, the base case makes about −4% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (−1.0% a year); bought at $2,610 psf it makes about +6%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.75M; about $2.81M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders no; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing yes; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: good.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: high. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Besides this project's own 515 homes, about 2,783 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Bayshore Drive (mixed-use GLS), Bagnall Haus, Bedok Rise (GLS), New Upper Changi Road (GLS)); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 579 resales a year.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: high. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (High concern)

Besides this project's own 515 homes, about 2,783 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Bayshore Drive (mixed-use GLS), Bagnall Haus, Bedok Rise (GLS), New Upper Changi Road (GLS)); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 579 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). That points to a real drag on resale prices around completion: plan to hold longer, and pick the unit types and stacks that stand out.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (High concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

77% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with many units left, late discounts or deals on slower stacks are possible as the deadline nears, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, New Upper Changi Road (GLS), is likely at about $3,230 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
