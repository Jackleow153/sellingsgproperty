Woodlands Drive 17 EC (Sim Lian): analysis and verdict

Woodlands Drive 17 (Woodlands South) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Buy only below $1,860 psf. A 3-bed here at about $1,740 to $1,840 psf is a little above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $1,770 psf as a new launch), so aim for the lower end. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 3-bed or 4-bed or 5-bed at or below $1,770 psf; a 3-bed of about 950 sq ft is about $1.68M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $1,860 psf (a 3-bed above about $1.77M). The tested $1,740 to $1,840 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2036 (9 years): the 5-year minimum occupation period applies.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Twin Fountains ($1,319 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $1,860 psf.
  • Watch: The Wynwood Grand (EC) launch nearby: likely at about $1,930 psf (our estimate). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Twin Fountains, Forestville, La Casa) work out to about $1,770 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (35% to 55% here): launches selling now in the OCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: Twin Fountains (845 m away, 418 homes, completed 2016): its 3-bed-size resales (about 810 to 1,090 sq ft) averaged $1,319 psf over the last 6 months (3 resales), against a median $1,331 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $1,792 psf is $473 psf (+36%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $1.49M.
  • Also note: The Wynwood Grand (EC) site nearby (269 m, land $782 psf ppr) is likely to launch at about $1,930 psf: more than this project's price.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the tested $1,792 psf, the base case makes about +41% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2036 (3.9% a year); bought at $1,770 psf it makes about +43%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $1.70M; about $2.54M by 2036 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders yes; HDB 4-room upgraders with some savings; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: good.
  • Cash needed under EC loan rules: At launch, a household at the $16,000 income ceiling can borrow at most about $1.01M under the 30% mortgage servicing ratio, so a 3-bed at $1,792 psf ($1.70M) needs about $750k in cash and CPF, including stamp duty.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 560 homes, about 778 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Wynwood Grand (EC), Norwood Grand); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 82 resales a year. Our test of 33 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the outer suburbs (11 km or more from the CBD): those with more than 1,817 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 4.0% a year over the past 4 years, against 4.3% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Moderate concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Wynwood Grand (EC), is likely at about $1,930 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

No Seller's Stamp Duty: the sale is after the 5-year minimum occupation period. Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): Twin Fountains 6.3%, Forestville 4.8%, La Casa 4.8%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.3% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst within reach of the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.

Questions

Is Clovelle of Woodlands a good investment?

At the tested $1,740 to $1,840 psf, the base case makes about +41% by 2036 after stamp duty and selling costs (3.9% a year). Bought at about $1,770 psf, in line with its closest competitors, it makes about +43%.

Can I sell before 2036?

Not on the open market. An executive condo must be lived in for 5 years from TOP before it can be sold to any Singaporean or PR (HDB). Its tender closed before 8 May 2026, so the 5-year rule applies; EC sites closing later have 10 years.
