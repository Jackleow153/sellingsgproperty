Chencharu Central Residences: analysis and verdict

Chencharu Close (Khatib, Yishun) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Walk away above $2,340 psf. A 3-bed here at about $2,300 to $2,450 psf is 6% above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,230 psf as a new launch). Buy only at or below about $2,230 psf. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed or 5-bed at or below $2,230 psf; a 3-bed of about 880 sq ft is about $1.96M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,340 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.06M). The expected $2,300 to $2,450 psf is partly above that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with The Estuary ($1,344 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (6)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,340 psf.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (The Estuary, North Park Residences, Skies Miltonia, The Miltonia Residences, Springleaf Residence) work out to about $2,230 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (30% to 42% here): launches selling now in the OCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out), and 10% for this project being integrated.
  • Latest transactions nearby: The Estuary (596 m away, 608 homes, completed 2013): its 3-bed-size resales (about 750 to 1,010 sq ft) averaged $1,344 psf over the last 12 months (6 resales), against a median $1,342 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,370 psf is $1,027 psf (+76%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $1.55M.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the expected $2,370 psf, the base case makes about +10% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2032 (1.9% a year); bought at $2,230 psf it makes about +17%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.09M; about $2.43M by 2032 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders with some savings; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: narrow.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 875 homes, about 110 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Yishun 10 Redevelopment); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 109 resales a year. Our test of 33 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the outer suburbs (11 km or more from the CBD): those with more than 1,817 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 4.0% a year over the past 4 years, against 4.3% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (High concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

No other land site nearby is waiting to launch, so there is no newer, cheaper launch to wait for here.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.2% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.4% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): The Estuary 6.5%, North Park Residences 4.9%, Skies Miltonia 3.4%, The Miltonia Residences 2.7%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise.
  • Bull (4.9% a year): Base plus 0.5 point as the Chencharu estate fills in (HDB plan, Confirmed); the North-South Corridor road opens from 2029 (Confirmed) but is not a new train line.

District

This district is in a steady, value phase: Yishun condos grew about 2.5% to 2.7% a year, and the private resale pool near Khatib is thin, so the exit depends on HDB upgraders.

Master Plan
