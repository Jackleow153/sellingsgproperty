The Collective at One Sophia: analysis and verdict

Sophia Road · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,840 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,060 psf as a new launch). 2-bed is the most wanted size in this rental and investment area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed at or below $3,060 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,125 sq ft is about $3.44M.
  • Best size here: 2-bed: this is an rental and investment area, where smaller units suit investors and tenants.
  • Walk away: Above $3,210 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.61M). The current average $2,840 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Sophia Hills ($2,288 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,210 psf.
  • Watch: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Sophia Hills, The M, 8 @ Mount Sophia) work out to about $3,060 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (16% to 56% here): launches selling now in the CCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.5 points more for each year of its age (29 launches against 104 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: Sophia Hills (188 m away, 493 homes, completed 2018): its 3-bed-size resales (about 960 to 1,290 sq ft) averaged $2,288 psf over the last 12 months (3 resales), against a median $2,109 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,839 psf is $551 psf (+24%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $2.35M.
  • Land bids nearby: The newest land bid nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS) (1,460 m, $1,865 psf ppr), is 31% above this site's land cost. It breaks even at about $2,990 psf, and analysts expect it to launch at $3,400-3,900 (PropNex, ERA, CBRE, Knight Frank).
  • Potential gain: Bought at the current average $2,839 psf, the base case makes about +12% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (2.9% a year); bought at $3,060 psf it makes about +4%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $3.19M; about $3.82M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders with some savings; HDB 4-room upgraders with some savings; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: good.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 367 homes, about 1,838 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Duet@Emily, Sophia Meadow, Dorset Gardens, Union Square Residences); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 1,089 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

27% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with many units left, late discounts or deals on slower stacks are possible as the deadline nears, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS), is likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (1.3% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (2.6% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): Sophia Hills 3.1%, 8 @ Mount Sophia 2.2%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise.
  • Bull (2.6% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
