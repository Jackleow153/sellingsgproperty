Parktown Residence: analysis and verdict

Tampines Street 62 · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,340 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,340 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,340 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,065 sq ft is about $2.49M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,460 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.62M). The current average $2,340 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2031 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with D'nest ($1,505 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (11)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,460 psf.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (D'nest, Coco Palms, The Palette, Nv Residences, Livia) work out to about $2,340 psf as a new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (29% to 47% here): launches selling now in the OCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.5 points more for each year of its age (29 launches against 102 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out), and 10% for this project being integrated.
  • Latest transactions nearby: D'nest (1,009 m away, 912 homes, completed 2017): its 3-bed-size resales (about 910 to 1,220 sq ft) averaged $1,505 psf over the last 6 months (11 resales), against a median $1,498 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,336 psf is $831 psf (+55%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $1.70M.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the current average $2,336 psf, the base case makes about +19% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2031 (3.5% a year); bought at $2,340 psf it makes about +18%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.49M; about $3.13M by 2031 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders with some savings; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: narrow.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 1,193 homes, no other new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it; the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 399 resales a year. Our test of 33 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the outer suburbs (11 km or more from the CBD): those with more than 1,817 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 4.0% a year over the past 4 years, against 4.3% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Low concern)

98% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with most units sold, there is little pressure to cut prices.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

No other land site nearby is waiting to launch, so there is no newer, cheaper launch to wait for here.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): D'nest 5.2%, Coco Palms 6.3%, The Palette 7.0%, Nv Residences 8.2%, Livia 6.4%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 6.6% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
