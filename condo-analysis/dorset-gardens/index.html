Dorset Gardens: analysis and verdict

Dorset Road (Farrer Park) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,600 to $2,800 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,880 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,880 psf; a 3-bed of about 950 sq ft is about $2.74M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $3,020 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.87M). The tested $2,600 to $2,800 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Piccadilly Grand ($2,450 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,020 psf.
  • Watch: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (Piccadilly Grand, Sturdee Residences, Soleil @ Sinaran) work out to about $2,880 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (6% to 41% here): launches selling now in the RCR charge about 3% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: Piccadilly Grand (338 m away, 407 homes, completed 2025): its 3-bed-size resales (about 810 to 1,090 sq ft) averaged $2,450 psf over the last 6 months (3 resales), against a median $2,386 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,699 psf is $250 psf (+10%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $3.18M.
  • Land bids nearby: The newest land bid nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS) (1,360 m, $1,865 psf ppr), is 39% above this site's land cost. It breaks even at about $2,990 psf, and analysts expect it to launch at $3,400-3,900 (PropNex, ERA, CBRE, Knight Frank).
  • Potential gain: Bought at the tested $2,699 psf, the base case makes about +26% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2032 (4.8% a year); bought at $2,880 psf it makes about +18%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.56M; about $3.44M by 2032 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders yes; HDB 4-room upgraders yes; Landed owners right-sizing yes; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: wide.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Besides this project's own 428 homes, about 1,553 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Duet@Emily, Sophia Meadow, The Collective at One Sophia, Bukit Timah Road (Newton GLS)); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 1,059 resales a year.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 428 homes, about 1,553 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Duet@Emily, Sophia Meadow, The Collective at One Sophia, Bukit Timah Road (Newton GLS)); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 1,059 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

This site's land ($1,338 psf ppr) cost −6% more than The Collective at One Sophia's ($1,426), which lifts the developer's break-even by about −5%. A 3-bed here at about $2,699 psf is −2% above The Collective at One Sophia's $2,750 launch average; private prices have risen about +5% since then (URA index), so in today's money you pay about 6% less than its buyers did, while the developer's costs rose more: the developer absorbs most of the higher land bid. Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS), is likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year), widened to the nearest suitable condos: Kerrisdale (0.9 km), Amaryllis Ville (0.9 km): Kerrisdale 6.3%, Amaryllis Ville 5.1%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.7% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site: Farrer Park MRT has been open since 2003, so the bull case equals the base case.
