River Valley Green (Parcel C): analysis and verdict

River Valley Green (Great World) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $3,300 to $3,500 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,490 psf as a new launch). 2-bed is the most wanted size in this rental and investment area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $3,490 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,000 sq ft is about $3.49M.
  • Best size here: 2-bed: this is an rental and investment area, where smaller units suit investors and tenants.
  • Walk away: Above $3,660 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.66M). The expected $3,300 to $3,500 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Riviere ($3,026 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,660 psf.
  • Watch: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (River Modern, Riviere, Martin Modern, Irwell Hill Residences, Oue Twin Peaks) work out to about $3,490 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (14% to 37% here): launches selling now in the CCR charge about 9% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out). A launch nearby from the last 2 years counts for half: it is the most direct evidence of what new homes here cost today.
  • Latest transactions nearby: Riviere (206 m away, 455 homes, completed 2023): its 3-bed-size resales (about 750 to 1,250 sq ft) averaged $3,026 psf over the last 6 months (3 resales), against a median $2,851 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $3,399 psf is $373 psf (+12%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $3.53M.
  • Also note: The Peck Hay Road (GLS) site nearby (1,784 m, land $1,865 psf ppr) is likely to launch at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf: more than this project's price.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the expected $3,399 psf, the base case makes about +6% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2032 (1.1% a year); bought at $3,490 psf it makes about +3%.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 470 homes, about 1,348 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (River Modern, River Opus, Orchard Boulevard (2026 GLS site), Sophia Meadow); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 1,190 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

This site's land ($1,730 psf ppr) cost +21% more than The Collective at One Sophia's ($1,426), which lifts the developer's break-even by about +18%. A 3-bed here at about $3,399 psf is +24% above The Collective at One Sophia's $2,750 launch average; private prices have risen about +5% since then (URA index), so in today's money you pay about 18% more than its buyers did, about as much as the developer's higher costs: the land bid is being passed on. Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Peck Hay Road (GLS), is likely at about $3,400 to $3,900 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

  • Bear (0.9% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (1.9% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year), widened to the nearest suitable condos: River Place (0.8 km): Oue Twin Peaks 0.1%, River Place 3.7%.
