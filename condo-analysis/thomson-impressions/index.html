Thomson Impressions: analysis and verdict

Bright Hill, D20 · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Walk away above $2,230 psf. A 3-bed here at about $2,280 psf is 8% above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,120 psf as a 8-year-old resale like this one). Buy only at or below about $2,120 psf. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this own-stay family area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,120 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,055 sq ft is about $2.24M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an own-stay family area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,230 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.35M). The recent resale average $2,280 psf is above that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Thomson Three ($2,256 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (4)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,230 psf.
  • Watch: The Lorong Puntong (Sin Ming Avenue GLS) launch nearby: likely at about $3,000 to $3,100 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: high. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (High concern)

Besides this project's own 283 homes, about 1,408 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Lorong Puntong (Sin Ming Avenue GLS), Thomson Reserve); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 276 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). That points to a real drag on resale prices around completion: plan to hold longer, and pick the unit types and stacks that stand out.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (High concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Lorong Puntong (Sin Ming Avenue GLS), is likely at about $3,000 to $3,100 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): Thomson Three 6.7%, Thomson Grand 4.6%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 5.7% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (5.7% a year): Base plus 1 point a year for the Cross Island Line (Phase 1) at Bright Hill station, Confirmed for 2030.
