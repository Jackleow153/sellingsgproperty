Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site): analysis and verdict

Holland Plain (off Old Holland Road) · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Low

Verdict: Buy only below $3,080 psf. A 3-bed here at about $3,000 to $3,100 psf is a little above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,930 psf as a new launch), so aim for the lower end. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,930 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,000 sq ft is about $2.93M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $3,080 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.08M). The expected $3,000 to $3,100 psf is partly above that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2032 (5 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Mayfair Modern ($2,295 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (5)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,080 psf.
  • Watch: The Dunearn Road (Turf City 2nd site) launch nearby: likely at about $3,000 to $3,300 psf (analysts). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: high. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. A cheaper new launch may come nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (High concern)

Besides this project's own 280 homes, about 1,474 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Amberwood at Holland, Dunearn House, Dunearn Road (Turf City 2nd site), Nava Grove); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 297 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). That points to a real drag on resale prices around completion: plan to hold longer, and pick the unit types and stacks that stand out.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Moderate concern)

This site's land ($1,491 psf ppr) cost +4% more than Amberwood at Holland's ($1,432), which lifts the developer's break-even by about +3%. A 3-bed here at about $3,049 psf is +1% above Amberwood at Holland's $3,019 launch average; private prices have risen about 0% since then (URA index), so in today's money you pay about the same as its buyers did, while the developer's costs rose more: the developer absorbs most of the higher land bid. Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS). That pushes them to price to sell at launch; slow sellers sometimes offer deals near the deadline, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Moderate concern)

The next launch nearby, Dunearn Road (Turf City 2nd site), is likely at about $3,000 to $3,300 psf, close to this project's 3-bed. A cheaper neighbour later could cap this project's resale price; compare both before committing.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
