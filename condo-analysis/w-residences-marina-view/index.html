W Residences Marina View: analysis and verdict

Marina View / Downtown, D01 · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Walk away above $2,970 psf. A 3-bed here at about $3,250 psf is 15% above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,830 psf as a new launch). Buy only at or below about $2,830 psf. 2-bed is the most wanted size in this rental and investment area. The 4-bed has a very thin resale pool: buy only to live in.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed at or below $2,830 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,220 sq ft is about $3.45M.
  • Best size here: 2-bed: this is an rental and investment area, where smaller units suit investors and tenants.
  • Walk away: Above $2,970 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.62M). The current average $3,250 psf is above that.
  • Own stay only: The 4-bed: few buyer groups could fund it today, so buy it only to live in long term.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with V On Shenton ($1,853 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (3)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,970 psf.
  • Watch: The Marina Gardens Lane (2026 GLS site) launch nearby: likely at about $2,730 psf (our estimate). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (V On Shenton, Marina One Residences, One Shenton, The Clift, Altez) work out to about $2,830 psf as a new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (29% to 44% here): launches selling now in the CCR charge about 7% over a just-completed resale and about 2.5 points more for each year of its age (29 launches against 101 of their resale competitors, this one and the 7 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: V On Shenton (87 m away, 510 homes, completed 2017): its 3-bed-size resales (about 1,040 to 1,400 sq ft) averaged $1,853 psf over the last 12 months (3 resales), against a median $1,854 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $3,253 psf is $1,400 psf (+76%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $1.95M.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the current average $3,253 psf, the base case makes about −19% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (−5.0% a year); bought at $2,830 psf it makes about −6%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $3.97M; about $3.45M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders no; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing no; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: narrow.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. A cheaper new launch may come nearby. Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. The price is above what same-size competitors support, whatever the land cost. A cheaper new launch may come nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 683 homes, about 1,288 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (51 Anson, Newport Residences, Union Square Residences, Marina Gardens Lane (2026 GLS site)); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 350 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (High concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

2% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with many units left, late discounts or deals on slower stacks are possible as the deadline nears, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Moderate concern)

The next launch nearby, Marina Gardens Lane (2026 GLS site), is likely at about $2,730 psf, below this project's 3-bed. A cheaper neighbour later could cap this project's resale price; compare both before committing.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (-2.9% a year): The competitors' own fall over the past 4 years carries on: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (0.0% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): V On Shenton 0.3%, Marina One Residences -6.1%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages a fall of 2.9% a year; a fall carried on for 10 years is not a fair middle case, so the base case is flat and the bear case keeps the fall going.
  • Bull (0.0% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
