8@BT: analysis and verdict

6 / 8 Bukit Timah Link · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Buy only below $2,720 psf. A 3-bed here at about $2,680 psf is a little above what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $2,590 psf as a new launch), so negotiate or choose a cheaper stack. 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $2,590 psf; a 3-bed of about 1,000 sq ft is about $2.59M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $2,720 psf (a 3-bed above about $2.72M). The current average $2,680 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with Mayfair Modern ($2,295 psf, 3-bed size, last 12 months (5)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $2,720 psf.
  • Watch: The Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site) launch nearby: likely at about $3,150 psf (our estimate). It sets the next price benchmark for this area.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 158 homes, about 212 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Amberwood at Holland); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 406 resales a year. Our test of 19 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the middle ring (6 to 11 km from the CBD): those with more than 862 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.9% a year over the past 4 years, against 3.6% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Moderate concern)

This site's land ($1,343 psf ppr) cost −6% more than Amberwood at Holland's ($1,432), which lifts the developer's break-even by about −5%. A 3-bed here at about $2,675 psf is −11% above Amberwood at Holland's $3,019 launch average; private prices have risen about 0% since then (URA index), so in today's money you pay about 11% less than its buyers did, while the developer's costs rose more: the developer absorbs most of the higher land bid. Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Moderate concern)

74% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with many units left, late discounts or deals on slower stacks are possible as the deadline nears, but the best stacks rarely get cheaper.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

The next launch nearby, Holland Plain (Sim Lian, 2nd site), is likely at about $3,150 psf, above this project's 3-bed. Waiting for it is unlikely to get you a cheaper new home here, and a dearer neighbour helps support this project's resale price.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.
