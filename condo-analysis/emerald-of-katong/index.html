Emerald of Katong: analysis and verdict

Jalan Tembusu · checked 25 Nov 2026 · confidence Medium

Verdict: Sold out: buy only as a sub-sale or a resale, at or below about $3,210 psf. At launch: Buy. A 3-bed here at about $2,420 psf is at or below what the closest competitors' 3-bed-size resales support (about $3,060 psf as a new launch). 3-bed and 4-bed are the most wanted sizes in this mixed own-stay and rental area.

Jack's recommendation

  • Target: 2-bed or 3-bed or 4-bed at or below $3,060 psf; a 3-bed of about 955 sq ft is about $2.92M.
  • Best size here: 3-bed and 4-bed: this is an mixed own-stay and rental area, and family sizes sell fastest (Thomson Reserve's 3- and 4-bedders sold out at launch; its 2-bedders were the slowest).
  • Walk away: Above $3,210 psf (a 3-bed above about $3.07M). The launch average $2,420 psf is within that.
  • Hold: Plan to keep it until about 2030 (4 years): Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years.
  • Before you book: Compare your unit's psf with The Shore Residences ($2,194 psf, 3-bed size, last 6 months (10)) and ask for the price of each stack; a higher floor or better facing should not push you past $3,210 psf.

Key points

  • What the closest competitors support: The closest competitors (The Shore Residences, Silversea, Waterbank At Dakota) work out to about $3,060 psf as a brand-new launch, after adding 5% where they were not built under the harmonised floor-area rules, a new-launch premium that rises with each competitor's age (34% to 36% here): launches selling now in the RCR charge about 3% over a just-completed resale and about 2.6 points more for each year of its age (30 launches against 107 of their resale competitors, this one and the 6 most extreme pairs left out).
  • Latest transactions nearby: The Shore Residences (464 m away, 408 homes, completed 2014): its 3-bed-size resales (about 810 to 1,100 sq ft) averaged $2,194 psf over the last 6 months (10 resales), against a median $2,186 psf for all sizes. A 3-bed here at about $2,415 psf is $221 psf (+10%) above that; a 3-bed there sold for a median $2.95M.
  • Potential gain: Bought at the launch average $2,415 psf, the base case makes about +43% after stamp duty and selling costs by 2030 (9.4% a year); bought at $3,060 psf it makes about +13%.
  • Who can buy it from you later: Who can buy a 3-bed from you (tested at today's $2.31M; about $3.51M by 2030 in the base case): HDB 5-room upgraders yes; HDB 4-room upgraders no; Landed owners right-sizing yes; Condo owners nearby moving or upgrading yes. Resale pool: wide.
  • Supply and land prices: Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby. Besides this project's own 846 homes, about 333 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Meyer Blue, Arina East Residences); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 991 resales a year.

Supply and land prices: should you worry?

Supply risk here: low. You are not paying for the developer's land bid: you pay what same-size competitors support. No cheaper new launch is coming nearby.

Will too many new condos nearby hurt my price? (Low concern)

Besides this project's own 846 homes, about 333 new homes within 2 km launch within about 2 years of it (Meyer Blue, Arina East Residences); the condo resale market within 2 km handles about 991 resales a year. Our test of 27 condos completed 2016 to 2022 in the city and city fringe (under 6 km from the CBD): those with more than 2,367 other new homes completing within 2 km and 2 years grew 1.5% a year over the past 4 years, against 0.5% for those with fewer (URA caveats). Supply nearby is not a big concern here.

The developer paid more for the land. Am I paying more? (Low concern)

Across 16 launches since 2023, launch prices rose roughly dollar for dollar with break-even (about $103 per $100), so developers kept a similar dollar margin while their percentage margin shrank (22% above break-even on land of $1,200 psf ppr or more, 31% on cheaper land). Part of that is location: dearer land is usually where homes sell for more anyway. So a high bid does show up in prices, but only as far as buyers will pay against nearby homes. The test that protects you is the price against same-size competitors: our buy-at and walk-away prices.

Will the developer cut prices later? (Low concern)

99.8% sold so far. Developers must sell every unit within 5 years of buying the land or repay most of a 40% stamp duty (IRAS); with most units sold, there is little pressure to cut prices.

Should I wait for the next launch nearby? (Low concern)

No other land site nearby is waiting to launch, so there is no newer, cheaper launch to wait for here.

What about supply across Singapore? (Moderate concern)

Islandwide, about 18,296 private homes complete in 2027 and 2028 and 15,810 homes in the pipeline were still unsold at mid-2026 (URA). That is enough to keep price growth moderate, but local supply and the local buyer pool matter more for your resale.

  • Premium over closest resale: +6%. A 3-bed here against the competitors' 3-bed-size resales (after the 5% area adjustment). Launches selling now averaged +27% over theirs (30 launches; +19% in the RCR, 9 launches). Below what the market usually pays.
  • Rental yield at this price: 2.9% gross. Competitors' median rent about $5.85 psf a month (URA rental contracts, 26q1, 26q2, 26q3, 26q4); a 3-bed rents for about $7,220 a month.

  • Sales pace: About 35 a month. 844 of 846 homes sold in about 24 months since launch; 2 left, about 0 months at that pace.

Potential gain

Potential gain is after Buyer's Stamp Duty at IRAS rates, about $6,000 legal fees and a 2% agent fee plus 9% GST when you sell. Loan interest, property tax, maintenance and rent are not included.

  • Bear (2.4% a year): Half the base case: competing supply at the exit, or a softer market.
  • Base (4.7% a year): The average yearly 3-bed price change over the past 4 years (URA caveats) of the competitors with a suitable record (at least 10 resales in the past year): The Shore Residences 7.0%, Silversea 6.0%, Waterbank At Dakota 5.6%. That period included the 2022–2023 rise. That averages 6.2% a year, above the market's 10-year pace, so the base case is capped at 4.7% (URA non-landed index, 2016 to 2026): 2022 to 2026 included an unusual rise, and a new launch bought at a premium rarely outgrows the market for years.
  • Bull (4.7% a year): No new Confirmed catalyst near the site in the holding period, so the bull case equals the base case.
