Mandatory for every condo analysis page (new launch or resale). Read it before writing or updating any analysis.

**The one rule:** facts are the evidence, not the product. Every page must reach a clear, opinionated conclusion and show the reasoning that got there, the way a top advisor would talk to a client they respect. Plain words. Explain every technical term the first time it appears (e.g. "psf ppr = land price per square foot of buildable floor area").

---

## 1. The verdict (top of the page, always)

Pick ONE verdict per unit type. A 2-bed and a 4-bed in the same project can deserve different answers.

| Verdict | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Buy** | At the expected price, the base case clears 5% a year net (section 3), and the scorecard is 7.5 or more. |
| **Buy only below $X psf** | Good project, but the price is the problem. Name the number. |
| **Own-stay only** | Good to live in, but the maths won't deliver 5% a year. Say who it suits. |
| **Walk away** | Base case below about 3% a year net, or a structural flaw (exit buyers can't afford it, heavy competing supply at exit, poor layout efficiency, etc.). |

The verdict card must contain:
- the verdict in one sentence, with the number ("Buy the 3-bed only at or below $2,450 psf; walk away above $2,600 psf")
- the **3 reasons** that decide it (the strongest evidence, not a list of everything)
- **who it suits** and **who should skip it**
- **what would change my mind**: 2–3 specific triggers (e.g. "if the 3-bed launches above $2,600 psf", "if the CRL station date slips past 2032")

"It depends" is not a verdict. If the data is thin, still give the verdict, mark its confidence (High / Medium / Low) and say what data would raise it.

## 2. Buy-at and walk-away prices (even before prices are out)

Work out three numbers, show the working, then decide.

**A. Developer floor price** (the lowest the developer can realistically sell at)
- Land cost psf ppr + construction cost + finance, fees and marketing + a normal developer margin.
- Use the construction cost from a current Singapore cost report (e.g. Rider Levett Bucknall or Arcadis) and analysts' breakeven estimates where published. Label it Estimate.
- Below this price, a launch is unrealistic.

**B. Fair value today** (what the market pays for comparable new homes)
- Start from recent new launches and the newest resale condos nearby, then adjust for:
  - age and remaining lease
  - distance to the MRT (measured walking time)
  - whether it's an integrated development
  - project size, unit size and layout efficiency
- Show each adjustment as a line with its reasoning.

**C. The 5% ceiling** (the highest price at which the base case still earns 5% a year net)
- Work backwards from the base-case exit price (section 3).

**How to decide**
- **Buy-at price** = the lower of B and C.
- **Walk-away price** = the entry price at which the base-case net return drops to about 3% a year.
  - Around the cost of a mortgage, the investment stops paying for its own borrowing.
  - Never set it above B plus a justified premium.
- **If C is below A,** the developer will almost certainly price above the level that earns 5%. Say so plainly: "At any realistic launch price, this won't deliver 5% a year. Buy it only to live in."

State both prices in psf AND as quantum (total price) for each main unit type.

---

## 3. The 5% test (show the maths, every time)

**Timeline**
- From purchase at launch, to TOP (about 4–5 years), to the earliest sale without Seller's Stamp Duty.
- For homes bought on or after 4 July 2025, SSD applies for 4 years. Verify the current rules before each update.

**Costs included**
- Buyer's Stamp Duty
- legal fees
- selling agent fee + GST
- interest paid during progressive payments
- property tax and maintenance after TOP
- rental income, if rented

**Two returns, always shown side by side**
1. **Price return:** psf growth per year, net of buying and selling costs.
2. **Return on your cash:** annualised return on the cash and CPF actually put in, with the loan. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses; say so.

**The key fact to tell readers:** because of stamp duty and selling costs, a 5% net return needs roughly **6.1–6.3% a year in price growth**.

Worked example: 1,000 sq ft unit, BSD at current rates, 2% + GST selling fee, about $6k legal fees, no interest or rent.

| Entry psf | BSD | Exit psf needed after 5 yrs | Exit psf needed after 6 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2,300 | $84,600 | $3,118 | $3,274 |
| $2,400 | $89,600 | $3,255 | $3,418 |
| $2,500 | $94,600 | $3,392 | $3,562 |
| $2,600 | $99,600 | $3,529 | $3,706 |

Recalculate for the real project. Then ask the only question that matters: **will a 4–6-year-old condo here realistically trade at that psf?** Answer it with the evidence in sections 4–6.

**Scenarios**
- **Base case growth** = what comparable projects actually achieved (section 5). Not a guess, and not the developer's story.
- **Bear case** = about half the base case, or flat if supply is heavy.
- **Bull case** = base case plus a premium, ONLY for a catalyst that is Confirmed (section 7).

**Result:** Pass / Borderline / Fail, plus the entry price that would turn it into a Pass.

## 4. District deep dive

Give a summary first, then the detail.

**Price trend:** 10-year condo resale psf trend for the district and planning area (annualised). Also the gap between new-launch psf and 5–10-year-old resale psf. A wide gap means new buyers are paying a premium that has to be earned back.

**Volume and liquidity:** transactions per year, and how fast resale units sell.

**Rents:** rent trend and gross yield for comparable units.

**Supply risk:** units launching within about 2 km in the next 3 years, and units reaching TOP around the same exit year. These are your competition when you sell.

**Verdict line:** "This district is in a rising / maturing / oversupplied phase, because…"

---

## 5. Competitor performance (similar size, similar age)

- Pick **3–5 condos** with a similar number of units, a similar unit mix and similar MRT access, within about 2 km or in the same planning area.
- Add **1–2 "best analogs"** elsewhere, e.g. other integrated developments, for an integrated project.

| What to show for each | |
|---|---|
| Basics | launch year and launch psf, TOP year, units |
| Performance | resale psf today, annualised gain from launch to today, and from launch to TOP + 1 year |
| Track record | % of resale transactions that made a profit, typical holding period |
| Layout | efficiency (strata area vs usable area, wasted space) |

Then explain **what drove the winners and the losers**: entry price, MRT opening, supply, size, layout. Finally, the bottom line for our project: "Comparable projects made X% a year. To earn 5%, this project must beat them by Y, which is likely / unlikely because…"

Side-by-side floor plans of the subject project vs the 2–3 closest competitors, wherever plans are available.

## 6. Future buyers and affordability (the exit test)

**Who buys from you at TOP + 1–2 years?**
- HDB upgraders (which towns, and their MOP waves: flats reaching Minimum Occupation Period near the exit year)
- private-to-private movers, PRs, investors, families chasing schools

**The affordability check**
- Exit quantum = base-case exit psf × size, for each main unit type.
- Household income needed:
  - TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio) 55%
  - MAS medium-term interest rate floor (verify current; it was 4%)
  - 75% loan, tenure per current rules
- Compare that income with the median household income of the likely buyer group.
- Typical upgrader capital = median 4-room / 5-room HDB resale price in the source towns − typical outstanding loan, plus CPF refund. Is it enough for the 25% down payment plus BSD?

**Conclusion:** what share of the natural buyer pool can afford your exit price. If the exit quantum outruns local upgraders, flag **exit risk** clearly.

## 7. URA Master Plan and transformation

- List every relevant plan within about 2 km, with distance, timeline and expected impact on value:
  - Master Plan 2019 (gazetted)
  - Draft Master Plan 2025
  - announced MRT lines and stations
  - regional and town centres, business parks, major malls, hospitals, schools
- **Label each:**
  - **Confirmed** (gazetted, or with a firm date)
  - **Planned** (in the Draft Master Plan, no date)
  - **Speculative** (talked about, not committed)
- Only Confirmed items may lift the base case. Planned items may go in the bull case.
- **If there is nothing significant, say so plainly:** "No major transformation is planned near this site. Any growth has to come from the project itself and the wider market."

## 8. Scorecard (supports the verdict, doesn't replace it)

Jack's five scores, each out of 10; overall = plain average.

| Score | 3/10 looks like | 7/10 looks like | 10/10 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | Fringe area, few amenities, limited upgrade pool | Mature town, good amenities, solid upgrader pool | Town centre / regional hub, deep buyer pool, Confirmed catalyst |
| Entry price | Above fair value (2B) | At fair value | Clearly below fair value and near the developer floor |
| Exit plan | Exit quantum beyond the local buyer pool, heavy supply at exit | Affordable to most upgraders, moderate supply | Wide, affordable buyer pool and little competing supply |
| Nearby food | Mostly a drive away | Hawker centre / food court within about 10 min walk | Hawker + mall food options within about 5 min walk |
| MRT proximity | > 12 min walk | 6–9 min walk | Directly connected / ≤ 3 min walk |

- Any score of 8 or more must cite its evidence.
- If the scorecard and the price test point different ways, the price test wins for investment buyers. Explain the difference in one line.

---

## 9. Evidence rules

- **Labels:** Confirmed / Reported / Estimate on every figure, with source and date.
- **Sources:** URA, HDB, IRAS, MAS, SingStat, developer and listed-company filings, then reputable research and press.
- **Portal listings and agent ads are not sources.**
- **Missing data:** say "Not available yet" and state how it would change the verdict. Never fill a gap with a guess presented as fact.
- **Opinions are welcome; invented numbers are not.** Every opinion must point to the evidence behind it.
- **No promises:** never write "guaranteed", "confirm profit" or similar. Projections are scenarios with stated assumptions.
