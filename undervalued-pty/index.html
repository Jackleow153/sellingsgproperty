3 Things Buyers Should Check

  1. How many years are left on the lease? A leasehold condo can show a large price gap because the lease is shorter. On this screen, a clean match asks for 70 years or more on a leasehold buy-low unit. Read the lease on the specific unit before you treat the gap as a reason to offer.
  2. How close are the two projects, and how many sales support the median? A clean match keeps the pair within 1.5 km and uses at least 4 sales on each side. A wide gap from a few transactions, or from a project at the other end of the district, is a weaker comparison for a resale or new-launch decision.
  3. Does the gap survive when the homes are compared at the same size? Sort by “Biggest gap at equal size”. If the raw dollar gap shrinks once the psf difference is applied to the older unit’s size, the lower price is mostly a smaller home. Buyers comparing resale with a post-launch project should read that size-adjusted gap beside the headline dollar gap.
How this screen works

The Condo Buy-Low Screen lists older private condos and apartments in Singapore whose median resale price sits more than $100,000 below a newer project in the same postal district. The age gap is 10 years or less, and both sides are the same bedroom type. The figures are medians of URA private residential caveats from November 2025 to October 2026. October 2026 is a part month.

What a buy-low gap means

A buy-low gap is the dollar difference between two median prices. It tells an HDB upgrader or a condo buyer where an older resale home sold for less than a newer resale condo, or for less than a post-launch project that is still recording developer sales. The gap is a comparison of recorded sales. The next question is whether the cheaper project is the right home, not simply the lower number.

How the comparison works

The resale view uses resale caveats only. The post-launch view takes the median new-sale price of projects with developer sales in the same window, then pairs them with resale condos up to 10 years older in the same district. URA caveats do not state a bedroom count, so this screen reads 2-bedder, 3-bedder and 4-bedder from the unit size and the era of the building. The size-adjusted gap applies the price-per-square-foot difference to the older unit’s floor area. A home that is cheaper only because it is smaller falls out of the clean matches.

Who it helps

Use it when you already know the district and the bedroom count. That includes an HDB upgrader comparing a resale condo with a new launch, and a buyer choosing between two private projects that are close in age. Each card is a shortlist. Check the remaining lease, the stack and floor, and whether a unit of that size is still for sale.

How the list was built

  • Data: every URA caveat for private condos and apartments with a sale date from Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, pulled from URA's property market search on 24 Oct 2026. October is a part month. Executive condominiums and bulk purchases are excluded.
  • Resale view: uses resale caveats only. Sub sales (resales before completion) are not used.
  • Post-launch view: a post-launch condo is any project with developer (new sale) caveats in the window. Its price is the median new-sale price for that bedroom type. It is compared with resale condos in the same district that are up to 10 years older.
  • Tenure and age: leasehold pairs compare lease start years, as your HDB board does. Freehold pairs compare completion years, taken from the year the MCST was registered. A freehold post-launch project counts as 2026. "Freehold cheaper than newer leasehold" compares completion years; a leasehold condo with no MCST record is dated lease start + 4 years. A freehold condo is never paired as the comparator for a leasehold one.
  • Clean match passes five checks: at least 4 sales on each side; the gap is still over $100k at equal size (psf difference × the buy-low unit's size); the two condos are within 1.5 km; both are the same URA type (condo vs condo, apartment vs apartment); and a leasehold buy-low unit has 70+ years left.

  • What the data can't see: MRT walking time, an attached mall, facilities, stack, floor and view, en bloc potential, and launch discounts or furnishing packages. New-sale prices also carry a premium for being new, which shrinks once a project completes. Treat each card as a lead to inspect.

Questions buyers ask

What is a buy-low gap on a Singapore condo?

A buy-low gap is the dollar difference between two median prices. On this screen an older private condo or apartment is at least $100,000 below a newer project in the same postal district, the age gap is 10 years or less, and both sides are the same bedroom type. The prices are medians of URA private residential caveats from November 2025 to October 2026.

How does this screen compare resale condos with new launches?

The resale view compares resale medians. The post-launch view compares those resale medians with the median developer new-sale price of projects that recorded new sales in the same window. Bedroom type is read from unit size, because URA caveats do not list bedrooms. The size-adjusted gap applies the psf difference to the older unit’s floor area.

What should a buyer check before acting on a condo price gap?

Check the remaining lease, the distance between the two projects, and how many sales support each median. A clean match on this screen asks for 70 or more years on a leasehold buy-low unit, a distance within 1.5 km, and at least 4 sales on each side. Also read the size-adjusted gap, so a lower price that comes only from a smaller home is not treated as a bargain.

How do I find undervalued Singapore property?

Choose Condos or HDB in the filter. For condos, leave Clean matches on, choose a bedroom type, and read each card. Undervalued for a condo means an older resale whose median price is more than $100,000 below a newer resale or post-launch project in the same district, with the same bedroom type and an age gap of 10 years or less. For HDB, choose a flat type or search a town. A card compares the same town and flat type, with a median gap above $100,000. Then read the size-adjusted gap before treating the lower price as a bargain.

Are the prices on this page asking prices?

No. Condo figures are medians of URA private residential caveats from November 2025 to October 2026. October 2026 is a part month. HDB figures are registered resale transactions, not asking prices, and they are not mixed into the condo medians.

Are HDB prices mixed into the condo comparison?

No. The HDB cards are a separate section. A card compares the same town and flat type, with lease years 1 to 10 apart and a median gap above $100,000. Those registered resales are not mixed into the URA condo medians.
