Condos and HDB · URA resales Nov 2025 – Oct 2026

Find Undervalued Singapore Property

Condos priced below their closest like-for-like competitors, district by district.

You cannot control the market, but you can control the price you pay, and that price decides much of what you make when you sell. Every purchase starts behind: stamp duty, legal fees and agent fees come off the top before any growth. Pay 10% too much for a $2 million condo and that is $200,000 to earn back before you make a cent. Yet similar homes a few streets apart can sell 10% to 20% apart, for reasons that often have little to do with the homes themselves: a quiet month, a seller who must move, a launch priced to sell fast. This page finds those gaps, so you can buy with room to spare instead of paying full price. Not every cheaper home is a bargain, so each one comes with the evidence to check before you commit.

3 Things Buyers Should Check

  1. Is there a reason it is cheaper? A price gap that tenants do not share (rents close to the neighbours') points to a cheap price. When rents are just as far below, the market may be pricing something real: a noisy road, older layouts, fewer facilities, or the wrong side of the area. The cards marked "Cheaper, check why" have this pattern. Visit at the hour you would come home.
  2. How much evidence is behind the average? Each card shows how many resales sit behind its price and its competitors'. URA records carry no floor level, so a few low-floor or unrenovated units can pull a small sample down. Ask for the floor and condition of the recent sales, and read the lease left on a 99-year condo.
  3. Does the gap hold for the unit you want? The gap is worked out per unit size. A condo can be cheap in its 3-bed size and fair in its 2-bed size. Use the "Price at that size" column as a ceiling for your offer, and open the competitors to see which condos set it.
How this screen works

The screen asks one question of every completed private condo with recent resales: is it selling below what its closest competitors' resales support? It uses the same rules as this site's condo analyses, so a resale condo and a new launch are judged the same way.

How each condo is compared

  1. Its own price. For each unit size it resells in (2-bed, 3-bed and 4-bed size, set from the unit area and the building's era because URA caveats carry no bedroom count), the average psf of its resales over the past 12 months, at least 3 of them, each moved to today with URA's private home price index. When the size range holds more than one layout, only resales within 15% of the most common size are used.
  2. Its competitors. Same tenure, same kind of condo (executive condos with executive condos, mall-integrated with mall-integrated), 0.4 to 2.5 times as many homes, completed 2004 or later, within 1 km; 2 km only if 1 km has fewer than 2. The 5 nearest are used.
  3. Like for like. Each competitor's average psf for its own resales within 15% of this condo's unit size (25% if too few), over the past 6 months, or 12 or 24 if too few, at least 3 resales, moved to today.
  4. Same age. Each competitor is moved to this condo's age: 1.8% a year for 99-year condos and 1.0% a year for freehold and 999-year condos. These are the price falls per year of building age measured across 1,406 99-year and 462 freehold pairs of nearby condos in the same data. The condo analyses' new-launch premium implies about 1.9% a year for 99-year condos, close to the measured figure.
  5. Fair price and gap. The plain average of the adjusted competitors is what they support. The gap is how far the condo's own average sits below it. A condo's overall gap weights each unit size by its number of resales.

The tests a condo must pass

Across all 527 unit sizes screened, the average gap is -0.4% and the usual spread is about 11%. So a single average a few percent below its neighbours means little. To be called undervalued, a condo must be at least 10% below overall, at least 3% below in every unit size compared, still 5% or more below when any one competitor is left out, cheaper than at least two in three competitors, and backed by at least 5 resales. A unit size far cheaper than the condo's other sizes (often penthouses or garden units with large terraces counted in the area) is shown but left out of the verdict.

Rents then split the result: Undervalued when both rent measures (median 3-bedroom rent and rent per square foot) are much closer to the neighbours' than the price is; Undervalued on price when one rent measure agrees and the other does not; Cheaper, check why when rents are as far below as the price. Cheaper, not proven is 6% or more below but short of one test (6% to 10% only, fewer than 5 resales, leaning on one competitor, or fewer than two in three competitors dearer). Condos 10% or more above their competitors are marked "Priced above its competitors" in the district tables.

Questions buyers ask

What makes a property undervalued?

A home is undervalued when it sells for clearly less than homes like it, nearby, are selling for. On this page that means a condo whose own resales over the past 12 months average at least 10% below what its closest like-for-like competitors support: same tenure, a similar number of homes, within 1 km (2 km if needed), the same unit size, adjusted for building age. It must also be cheaper at every unit size compared, stay 5% or more below when any one competitor is left out, be cheaper than at least two in three competitors, and rest on at least 5 resales. 18 of the 294 resale condos screened pass.

Why does buying below value matter?

Because the price you pay is the one part of your return you control. Stamp duty, legal fees and agent fees take a slice of every purchase, and Seller's Stamp Duty applies if you sell within 4 years. Paying 10% too much for a $2 million condo is $200,000 that has to be earned back before you make any profit. Buying 10% below the neighbours gives you that room instead: a cushion if prices soften, and a price the next buyer can accept.

Which condos are the most undervalued right now?

As of 27 Nov 2026, the resale condos furthest below their competitors that pass every test are Cityscape @Farrer Park (D08, about 23% below), Marina Bay Suites (D01, about 21% below), Parc Sophia (D09, about 19% below), Normanton Park (D05, about 18% below) and Grandeur 8 (D20, about 18% below). Among condos still selling, The Robertson Opus and The Orie are priced about 16% below their buy-at prices. These are findings from past sales, not a recommendation to buy: open each card to see the competitors and the sales behind the number.

Which districts have the most undervalued condos?

D09 (Orchard, River Valley) has the most, with three, followed by D01, D08, D19, D21 and D23 with two each. By region, 7 of 91 city-fringe (RCR) condos, 6 of 72 core central (CCR) condos and 5 of 131 suburban (OCR) condos screened came out undervalued, so suburban condos were about half as likely to be priced well below their neighbours.

Why would a condo sell for less than its neighbours?

Sometimes for a reason buyers should weigh: a busy road or expressway, an older or less efficient layout, fewer facilities, a longer walk to the MRT, a shorter lease, or many similar units coming up for sale nearby. Sometimes for reasons that have nothing to do with the home: a seller who has to move quickly, a thin resale market with few buyers that month, or a developer pricing a launch to sell fast. Rents help tell them apart. Tenants pay for the home, not the deal, so a condo whose rents are close to its neighbours' while its prices are not is more likely to be cheap for the second kind of reason.

Can an undervalued condo still lose money?

Yes. A lower price gives you a margin of safety, not a guarantee. The whole market can fall, a gap can stay open for years if buyers keep discounting the condo, and buying and selling costs still apply. Buy a home that suits how you will live in it or rent it out, at a price you can hold through a downturn. A gap on this page is a reason to look closer, not a reason to buy.

How do I check a condo before making an offer?

Ask for the recent caveats for the exact unit size and compare them with this page. Check the floor, facing and condition of those sales, because the URA records used here carry no floor level. Read the lease left and the maintenance fees, compare rents for the same bedroom type, and visit at the time of day you would come home. If the price still sits below its competitors after all that, the gap is likely real.

Which condos are compared with each other?

Only condos with the same tenure: 99-year leasehold with 99-year leasehold (leases of 90 to 110 years), freehold with freehold and 999-year, and executive condos only with executive condos. Mall-integrated condos are only compared with other mall-integrated condos. The competitor must have 0.4 to 2.5 times as many homes, be completed in 2004 or later, and sit within 1 km. If fewer than 2 qualify within 1 km, the search widens to 2 km. The 5 nearest are used.

How are different unit sizes and building ages handled?

For each unit size a condo resells in, every competitor's price is the average psf of its own resales within 15% of that size (25% if too few), over the past 6 months, or 12 or 24 if too few, moved to today with URA's price index. Each competitor is then adjusted to this condo's age: 1.8% a year for 99-year condos and 1.0% a year for freehold, the price fall per year of age measured across 1,868 nearby condo pairs in the same data.

Are new launches and post-launch condos included?

Yes. The 31 condos still selling developer units that have a condo analysis on this site are listed in their district. Each analysis sets a buy-at price from the same kind of competitors, converted to a new-launch price with the premium launches are paying now. A selling condo counts as undervalued when the developer's current average is 10% or more below that buy-at price; 6 do.

Why does the page show rents?

Rents are a check on the price gap. If tenants pay close to the neighbours' rent while buyers pay much less, the discount is in the price, not in the home. If rents are just as far below, the market may be pricing a real drawback, so those condos are marked "Cheaper, check why". Rents are URA rental contracts over the last 4 quarters: the median 3-bedroom rent and the median rent per square foot.

Can I find undervalued HDB flats too?

Yes. Choose HDB at the top. Each HDB card compares registered resale prices for an older lease and a newer lease in the same town and flat type, with lease start years 1 to 10 apart and a median gap above $100,000. Those prices come from HDB's open data on data.gov.sg and are never mixed into the condo comparison.

Are the prices on this page asking prices?

No. Resale prices are URA caveats lodged for completed private condos from November 2025 to October 2026. Selling-now prices are the developer's current average in each condo analysis. HDB figures are registered HDB resales.

How up to date is this list?

The resale prices cover Nov 2025 to Oct 2026 from URA caveats, and the page was last updated on 27 Nov 2026. Selling-now prices come from each condo analysis, dated on its card. A condo's position can change once a few new sales are lodged, so check the latest caveats before you make an offer.
